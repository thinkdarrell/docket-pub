# Meeting types name the body: a Birmingham committee recording stops reading "Regular City Council Meeting", every meeting carries a body and a session kind, and an admin correction survives ingest

This ExecPlan is a living document. The sections `Progress`, `Surprises & Discoveries`, `Decision Log`, and `Outcomes & Retrospective` must be kept up to date as work proceeds.

This document must be maintained in accordance with `plans/PLANS.md`.

## Purpose / Big Picture

A reader who opens https://docket.pub/al/birmingham/ in the week of 2026-10-19 sees six meetings, every one labelled "Regular City Council Meeting", on a Wednesday, two Thursdays, a Monday, a Tuesday, and another Wednesday. Only the Tuesday one is the council's regular meeting. The city's own calendar at https://www.birminghamal.gov/government/city-council names the others: the Economic and Workforce Development / Tourism Committee on Tuesday at 11:30, the Committee of the Whole / Administration and Governmental Affairs on Wednesday at 15:30, the Transportation and Infrastructure Committee the following Monday at 14:00. docket.pub shows the clerk's clip name, and the clerk names nearly every clip "Regular City Council Meeting".

After this plan, each meeting row carries three facts the site can show and filter on: `meeting_type`, which body met (`council`, `committee`, `planning`, `board`, `other`); `session_kind`, what kind of sitting it was (`regular`, `special`, `work_session`, `budget_hearing`); and `body_name`, the body's name when a source supplies it ("Committee of the Whole / Administration & Governmental Affairs", "Finance Committee"). A fourth column, `type_source`, says which evidence decided it (`title`, `calendar`, `schedule_rule`, `admin`), and an admin can set all three by hand on the meeting page and have the choice survive every later ingest. The meeting cards on the city page, the meetings list, and the home page's "This week" strip show a label built from those facts ("Committee of the Whole / Administration & Governmental Affairs", "City Council meeting", "Council committee meeting") instead of the raw clip name; the meeting page keeps the clip name beside it as provenance ("The city's archive lists this recording as “Regular City Council Meeting”."); the meetings list gains a filter row (All, Council, Committees, Planning, Boards, Other) on the `?type=` parameter the route already accepts. Homewood's and Vestavia Hills' rows, whose titles do name the body, stop being misfiled by the keyword order in the classifier: "City Council Regular Work Session" becomes a council work session rather than a council meeting, and "Special Called Special Issues Committee" becomes a committee rather than losing its body to the word "special".

How to see it working once the whole plan has shipped and the operator has run the one-statement reclassification on production: open https://docket.pub/al/birmingham/meetings/?type=committee and see the Monday, Wednesday, and Thursday recordings of 2026 listed under committee labels, with the Tuesday council meetings absent; open any of them and read the body name (where the city calendar supplied it) or "Council committee meeting" (where only the schedule rule applied), with the clip name quoted underneath.

## Decision Log

- Decision: two classification columns, `meeting_type` for the body and `session_kind` for the sitting, instead of adding values to the one column.
  Rationale: the one column today mixes bodies (`council`, `committee`, `planning`, `board`) with sittings (`special`, `work_session`), which is why "Special Called Finance Committee" loses its body and "City Council Regular Work Session" counts as a council meeting. A single richer vocabulary (`council_special`, `committee_special`, ...) would multiply every reader's predicate. The alternative of leaving `meeting_type` alone and adding only `body_name` would keep the misfiling.
  Date/Author: 2026-10-21, the author (Claude) with the operator's review findings.
- Decision: `meeting_type` keeps its name and its reader set; the vocabulary of `special` and `work_session` moves to `session_kind`, and the CHECK constraint that enforces the new vocabulary waits until M6, after the operator has reclassified production.
  Rationale: a CHECK added in M2 would fail on the rows that still hold `special` and `work_session`, and a migration that remaps them in SQL would restate the classifier's rules in a second place (`docs/PRINCIPLES.md` One owner per fact). Between M2 and M6 the old values are read by nothing that breaks: `?type=special` still filters, and the briefing predicate matches `council` either way.
  Date/Author: 2026-10-21, the author.
- Decision: the evidence ladder is admin lock, then calendar match, then schedule rule, then title; a specific title (one that says special, called, work session, budget, or names a committee or board) is not overridden by the calendar or the rule.
  Rationale: the title is the city's own document and is right whenever it is specific; it is wrong only in the one generic case, Birmingham's "Regular City Council Meeting" on every clip. The calendar is a second city source that names the body for upcoming weeks only; the rule is an inference and ranks last among the evidence, above only the generic title.
  Date/Author: 2026-10-21, the author.
- Decision: the schedule rule fires only when the title classifies as a regular council meeting, the city's metadata carries a `council_schedule` weekday, the row's weekday differs, the row has no minutes link, the row has no agenda items, and the title is not cancelled; it yields `committee`, `regular`, no body name, `type_source = 'schedule_rule'`.
  Rationale: the 2024-12-23 Monday council meeting (minutes linked) must stay council; a committee clip on Granicus has an agenda link but zero parsed items and never a minutes link. A row that later gains minutes or items is re-resolved on the next ingest, since `ingest_all` passes no `since` and re-upserts every row the feed lists.
  Date/Author: 2026-10-21, the author. Superseded the same day by the next entry: the no-items condition is dropped.
- Decision: the schedule rule does not require zero agenda items; its conditions are the generic title, a `council_schedule` weekday that differs from the row's, no minutes link, and a title that is not cancelled.
  Rationale: the review (2026-10-21) pointed out that the Committee of the Whole previews the next council agenda, so its clips can carry dozens of parsed items (meeting 2613 holds 51), and a past Wednesday clip the calendar never saw would have stayed "City Council meeting" under the old condition. A real council meeting moved to a Monday is still protected: it gains a minutes link once minutes are posted, and in the weeks before that the calendar rung, which outranks the rule, names it from the city's own "BIRMINGHAM CITY COUNCIL MEETING" event. The rows the rule touches that predate 2026 are listed one by one by the dry run so the operator reads them before the write.
  Date/Author: 2026-10-21, the author after the review.
- Decision: the three ingest writers set the classification columns on INSERT, and on UPDATE only when the row's `type_source` is `title` and it is not locked; `resolve_and_store` alone moves a row between rungs.
  Rationale: the review found that the first draft had `_upsert_meetings` writing `type_source = 'title'` on every daily run and `resolve_and_store` restoring the calendar or rule result seconds later, so a page loaded between the two stages, or a run that failed in Stage 3, would show the clip name again. With the writer gated on `type_source = 'title'`, a resolved row is never touched by the upsert; `resolve_and_store` recomputes the whole ladder from the current title each run, so a row that should fall back (minutes arrived, title gained "Cancelled") still does.
  Date/Author: 2026-10-21, the author after the review.
- Decision: the matched calendar event is stored on the meeting (`calendar_event_id`), not recomputed.
  Rationale: `Resolution` already carried the id and the first draft then threw it away and recomputed the match for the provenance link, which could return a different event or none once another event landed on the same day. One nullable foreign key makes the link a join and the stored `type_source = 'calendar'` self-consistent.
  Date/Author: 2026-10-21, the author after the review.
- Decision: when several calendar events fall on a meeting's day, the tie is broken by documents, never by `start_time`: a row with a minutes link or at least one agenda item takes the day's council-meeting event when exactly one exists; a row with neither takes the day's committee event when exactly one exists; otherwise the calendar rung yields nothing and the ladder continues.
  Rationale: `start_time` is wrong by construction (Surprises, issue #88): an archived clip stores 02:00 and an upcoming row stores a time two hours late, so the nearest-time rule in the first draft would have matched the Tuesday 09:00 council clip to the 11:30 committee as often as not. Birmingham holds committee meetings on council Tuesdays (the Economic and Workforce Development / Tourism Committee at 11:30 on 2026-10-20), where the schedule rule cannot help; the documents rule separates them because the council meeting has minutes or dozens of items and the committee clip has neither. Two committee clips on one day (Thursday 2026-10-15 has two) stay "Council committee meeting" by the schedule rule. Fixing the adapter's timestamp reading is a one-line change to `granicus.py` and a backfill of stored times, and it is left to its own change under issue #88 so this plan's scope stays the classification; the cause is recorded on the issue by the operator.
  Date/Author: 2026-10-21, the author after the review.
- Decision: the label keeps two facts the title carries that the columns do not: a cancelled title gets " (cancelled)" appended, and a work session of a non-council body gets " (work session)".
  Rationale: the card prints the label instead of the title after M5, and "Regular City Council Meeting - Cancelled (Next Regular Meeting 03/24/2026)" would have shown as "City Council meeting" with nothing cancelled about it; "Parks and Recreation Work Session" and "Parks and Recreation Board Meeting" would have printed the same label. The cancelled test is the regex `query.py` already owns for SQL (`CANCELLED_PREDICATE_SQL`); it gains a Python twin in the same file, `is_cancelled_title`, built from one pattern string, so the rule still has one owner, and the web layer passes the boolean to `label_for`, since `adapters/_helpers.py` may not import services.
  Date/Author: 2026-10-21, the author after the review.

## Idempotence and Recovery

Migrations 038 and 039 use `ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS`, `CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS`, and `ADD CONSTRAINT` guarded by a `DROP CONSTRAINT IF EXISTS` first, so re-applying is safe; their downs restore the prior shape and are exercised by their tests. The calendar upsert is keyed on `(municipality_id, source_event_id)`, so repeated runs change no count. `resolve_and_store` and the reclassification script write only rows whose classification differs and never a locked row, so a second run is a no-op, which the dry-run diagonal demonstrates. If the production reclassification table shows a transition the Context assumptions do not predict, the operator stops before the write, records it in Surprises, and the next session decides; nothing has been written. If a deploy of M4 precedes the operator's reclassification, the daily ingest resolves only the rows the feed lists that day, which for Granicus is every row, so Birmingham converges on its own within one run; the script remains the recorded, inspected path. Rolling back M5 is a code revert; rolling back M4 is a code revert plus `UPDATE meetings SET type_source = 'title' WHERE type_source IN ('calendar', 'schedule_rule')` and a reclassify, both one statement per city. Hidden rows are never touched by the script (it selects `is_hidden = FALSE`) and admin-locked rows are never touched by anything but the unlock route.

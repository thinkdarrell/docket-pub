# 0083 — A video vote within 15 seconds after an item's cue point is attached to the item before it

## Decision

A vote read off the meeting video (`votes.source = 'video_ocr'`) is attached to the agenda item whose cue point (`agenda_items.video_timestamp_seconds`) is the latest at or before the vote's second, except that a vote within `NEAR_CUE_S` seconds (15.0) of that cue is the close of the previous item's roll call and is attached to the latest item with a strictly earlier cue second: one step back, never repeated, items sharing a cue second skipped together, the confidence scored from the gap to the item attached to (`item_index_for_vote` and `match_votes_by_timestamp` in `src/docket/analysis/vote_matcher.py`). A stored attachment is never changed by the matcher; the ones written under the old rule are moved once by `scripts/rematch_near_cue_votes.py`, which deactivates the old row and inserts the new one and deletes nothing.

## Rationale

The old rule, the latest cue at or before the vote, is wrong at the one place a clerk is busiest. A roll call closes, the result appears on screen, and in the same second the clerk advances the player's index to the next item, so the vote's second lands on, or a few seconds after, the next item's cue point and the vote is filed under an item that has not started yet, with confidence 1.0 and nothing to flag it. GitHub issue #142 is the reproduction: on meeting 3184 (2026-10-27) vote 12396 at second 2256 is item 3's roll call (item 3 opened at 2145, its roll call ran 2191 to 2255, the clerk said "Item three passes. Next item." and then "Should be at 64"), but item 64's cue point is also 2256. Counted on production on 2026-11-07, 78 of the 237 attached video votes sat within 10 seconds of their item's cue point, 2 within 11 to 30 seconds, and 157 at 31 seconds or more: two humps with almost nothing between them, and every one of the 78 had an earlier cued item in its meeting.

The rule was settled by reading before it was changed. The operator read 79 of the 80 near-cue votes (within 30 seconds) against their meetings' transcripts, deciding from the words alone which item's roll call each display closed; the verdicts, vote by vote, are in the Decision Log of plan `video-votes-land-on-the-item-voted`. Seventy-one read as the earlier item, none at or under 15 seconds read as the cued item, seven read as neither, and one, at 30.0 seconds, read as the cued item. The largest gap that read as the earlier item was 11.0 seconds (vote 12345: "Item 14 passes. Next item. Should be item 15" at 1229 to 1232, the display read at 1235), so the threshold is 15 rather than the 10 the issue counted with: the round number between 11.0 and 30.0, and production held exactly two votes in that band, both read. The one `attached` reading, vote 1279, sits between two rows both titled "APPROVAL OF THE CONSENT AGENDA" cued two seconds apart, so it reads the same under either row.

One step back and not a walk: a consent agenda is read with cue points one or two seconds apart, and a repeated walk to the first item whose gap exceeds the threshold would carry a consent vote back through the whole run to the block's first item while the gap-based confidence lost its meaning; one step lands it on a consent item of the same run, which links to the same moment under the consent rule of `docs/specs/video-links.md`. Items sharing a second were one click of the clerk's, so they are skipped as one.

The alternatives, each rejected. Leaving a near-cue vote unattached, under "source or silence", when the evidence cannot say which of two items it belongs to: of the 78 readable votes at or under the threshold none named the cued item and 71 named the earlier one, so silence would have taken the item away from about a third of the video votes on the site where the source was clear on nearly all of them, and the daily `vote_matching` task would have revisited those meetings forever. Marking the link `needs_review` instead of moving it: `needs_review` is a column of `votes` with five OCR reasons, no reader of `vote_agenda_items` shows a per-link flag, and a half-confident wrong item is still the wrong item. Re-running the matcher over deactivated links to apply the new rule to stored rows: the matcher visits only votes with no active link and its upsert never sets `is_active`, so that would have re-created the unchanged majority as new rows beside their tombstones, 237 writes for 79 corrections, where computing the answer in the service and changing only the differing rows is the targeted statement decision `0026` requires. Deleting the old row instead of deactivating it: `is_active` exists for exactly this, every reader filters on it, and the ghost row keeps the record of what the old rule wrote.

The seven `unclear` readings are of one shape: the words name an item that is neither candidate, because the clerk clicked through one or two cue points before the display was read, and the one-step rule still lands those votes on an item the words do not name. They are four roll calls on four meetings, not moved by hand, and the first entries of `D26`'s trigger in `docs/DEBT.md`.

# Transcript comparison

What the desktop producer under `transcriber/` uploads for a Birmingham City Council meeting, how docket.pub compares what was said against the official minutes, and what happens to a finding a human has already reviewed when the comparison runs again. Present tense. This file covers the producer's output rules (`transcriber/transcriber/engine.py`, `prompt.py`, `db.py`, and `cli.py`) and the comparison stage, `run_compare_stage` in `src/docket/ai/transcripts/compare.py`; the rest of the transcript pipeline (the worker's speaker resolution, event extraction, retries, the review queue, the ship gate, alerts) and the operator procedures for running the producer are in `docs/runbooks/transcripts.md`.

## What the producer uploads

The producer runs faster-whisper's batched pipeline for speech recognition and pyannote 3.1 for diarization (deciding which stretches of audio are the same voice; each voice gets a label such as `SPEAKER_07`, called a cluster). Whisper emits chunks of roughly 28 seconds, each a `RawSegment` in `transcriber/transcriber/engine.py` carrying its text, Whisper's `avg_logprob` and `no_speech_prob`, and the word-level timestamps as `Word` values; pyannote emits `DiarTurn` values, one per stretch of one voice. `build_output()` in `engine.py` turns them into the upload in a fixed order: `clean_segments()`, then `split_by_speaker()`, then `insert_silence_markers()`, then one `Speaker` per cluster label a segment uses, then `validate()`; `speech_seconds` is computed over the kept chunks. The JSON file shape is unchanged (`CONTRACT_VERSION = 1` in `transcriber/transcriber/contract.py`).

A chunk is dropped as a hallucination when `is_hallucination()` says so, with this precedence: a chunk whose normalized text (`normalize_text()`: lowercase, letters and digits only, single spaces) is empty is dropped whatever its confidence; otherwise a chunk whose `avg_logprob` is `None` or above `HALLUCINATION_LOGPROB` (-0.9) is kept; otherwise it is dropped when it has at most `HALLUCINATION_MAX_WORDS` (8) normalized words or when `is_phrase_only()` finds it consists only of the stock phrases in `HALLUCINATION_PHRASES` in `engine.py` ("thank you", "thank you for joining us", "all right", "okay", and the rest of that tuple; the phrases are consumed from the left, always taking the longest match, so a repeated "Thank you. Thank you. Thank you." longer than the word cap is still dropped). `no_speech_prob` is not used. The rule runs on the raw chunks before the split, because the batched pipeline merges a genuine short utterance into the surrounding chunk, so a standalone short low-confidence chunk is almost always Whisper filling applause or silence, whereas after the split a genuine one-word "Here." would look the same.

A chunk is dropped as a prompt leak when `is_prompt_leak(text, initial_prompt)` finds any `PROMPT_LEAK_MIN_WORDS` (8) consecutive normalized words of it occurring, in order and on word boundaries, in the normalized initial prompt the engine was given; a chunk with fewer than eight words never matches, and an empty prompt never matches. `clean_segments()` tests the hallucination rule first so a chunk is counted once, returns the kept chunks and the counts `{"hallucination": n, "prompt_leak": m}`, and `build_output()` logs the counts at INFO when either is nonzero. On the 190 speech rows of the fixture `tests/fixtures/transcript_2543/segments.json`, run against the old `Terms:` prompt, the two rules drop seven hallucinations and one prompt leak (seqs 30, 38, 63, 75, 117, 124, 126, 164).

The initial prompt is prose, built by `build_initial_prompt()` in `transcriber/transcriber/prompt.py`: the lead `Birmingham City Council.`, then ` Councilors present are A, B, C.` when the meeting's roster has names, then the sentences of `VOCABULARY_SENTENCES` joined by single spaces, ten short sentences in transcript style with the proper nouns Whisper misspells first and the common words last. There is no `Terms:` list, because Whisper conditions on the prompt as if it were preceding transcript text and a labelled list is both unlike speech and easy to reproduce verbatim. The budget is 600 characters; whole sentences are dropped from the end until the prompt fits, so every surviving sentence stays grammatical, and with the nine-name 2026 roster the first seven sentences fit (551 characters). A roster that alone exceeds the budget raises `PromptBudgetError`, and `process_claim()` in `transcriber/transcriber/cli.py` then uses the vocabulary-only prompt.

The split assigns a cluster to every word and cuts each chunk wherever the cluster changes. `assign_word_clusters()` gives a word the label of the diarization turn it overlaps most; a word overlapping no turn takes the nearest turn's label when that edge is within `WORD_SNAP_S` (1.0 second), otherwise the previous word's label, and a leading run of unlabelled words takes the first label found; a word with no turn anywhere near and no earlier label stays `None`, exactly as `assign_clusters()` leaves an unmatched chunk. Candidate turns are bounded with two bisects over the turns sorted by start, so the cost is proportional to words plus turns. There is no smoothing of short runs: a one-word run is a legitimate turn, which is what makes the roll call (nine names, nine answers of "here") come out as separate segments that `find_roll_call_pairs()` in `src/docket/ai/transcripts/speakers.py` can pair. `split_by_speaker()` emits one `Segment` per run of same-cluster words: the first run starts at the chunk's `start_s`, the last ends at its `end_s`, interior boundaries come from the word timestamps, and `end_s` is clamped to at least `start_s`; a run's text concatenates the raw word pieces and collapses whitespace, which keeps `O'Quinn` together because faster-whisper carries the leading space inside each word and emits an apostrophe piece with none; a run whose text is empty is skipped before the edges are assigned. Every child inherits the chunk's `avg_logprob` and `no_speech_prob`, because the two numbers are Whisper statistics of the chunk and there is no honest per-child value. A chunk with no words keeps the whole-chunk assignment: `assign_clusters()` gives it the label of the one turn that overlaps it most.

`process_claim()` in `cli.py` calls `clean_segments()` right after `engine.transcribe()`, so the low-speech gate (`is_low_speech()`, `LOW_SPEECH_FLOOR_S` of 180 seconds) measures the cleaned chunks; `build_output()` then cleans again, and that second pass drops nothing in the loop (it is there for `dry_run()` and the tests). `words.json` is written beside `transcript.json` under the meeting's archive folder from `engine.last_words`, which the engine filled while transcribing, before any cleaning, so it still holds the dropped chunks' words; it is not uploaded (migration 035 keeps word timestamps out of Postgres) and only its path travels on the output as `words_path`.

`TRANSCRIPT_VERSION` in `engine.py` is the number `transcripts.version` carries for an upload: 1 is whole-chunk clusters with no cleaning (the 48 uploads made before 2026-10), 2 is the per-word speaker split, the hallucination and prompt-leak filters, and the prose prompt. It is bumped when the producer's output changes shape or content again, and it is distinct from `CONTRACT_VERSION`, which names the JSON file's shape. The producer package `__version__` is `0.2.1`: `0.2.0` from the same change, `0.2.1` from the re-run fixes (the old transcript stays public through a re-run, a failed re-run restores the row, the exit code, the busy guard's single writer), which left the output unchanged.

A meeting is re-transcribed with `--meeting-id N`, which `claim_meeting()` in `transcriber/transcriber/db.py` serves under the same advisory lock as `claim_next()`. `_MEETING_SQL` selects the meeting only when it is a Birmingham meeting with a video URL, not hidden, with a numeric Granicus id; when no row comes back `claim_meeting()` returns `None` and the producer logs `meeting N is not an eligible Birmingham meeting` and exits 1. When the row's `last_attempted_at` is within the last 30 minutes it raises `MeetingBusy` before any write and the producer logs `meeting N was touched at T; wait 30 minutes` and exits 1: the worker's `advance()` in `src/docket/ai/transcripts/pipeline.py` writes `status` with no guard on the status it claimed at, so a stage in flight would overwrite the claim, and a compare finishing in that window would clear the marker below; the worker's claim (`CLAIM_SQL` in the same file) stamps `last_attempted_at = now()` and no stage runs 30 minutes, so the guard closes the window without touching the Railway side, and `docs/runbooks/transcripts.md` says not to start a re-run inside the worker's daily 10:00 Central window. That claim is the column's only writer: the producer's `mark_status()` never writes `last_attempted_at`, so a second `--meeting-id` right after a failed re-run is not refused, and a fresh upload reaches the worker with the column NULL, so the 24-hour backoff in `CLAIM_SQL` (`last_attempted_at IS NULL OR last_attempted_at < now() - interval '24 hours'`) no longer delays a freshly uploaded transcript to the following day's tick; the worker's own backoff rule is unchanged. Otherwise a meeting with no `transcripts` row gets one at `claimed`, and an existing row is first read whole when it had been uploaded (`uploaded_at IS NOT NULL`) and carried on the returned `Claim` as `prior`, then set to `claimed` with `stage_attempts = 0` and `last_attempted_at`, `needs_review_reason`, and `last_error` NULL, and with `compare_skipped_reason = 'transcript_changed'` only when `uploaded_at IS NOT NULL`, because a never-uploaded row was never compared and its stored reason is then the honest one. The marker is what makes the worker's compare run as a changed-input pass (Re-comparison below); a re-uploaded meeting that is later held and retried from the admin queue loses it (`D52` in `docs/DEBT.md`). With `--meeting-id` the loop runs one claim and ignores `--since` and `--limit`, and `main()` exits 1 whenever the `done` line's `uploaded` count is 0 (not eligible, busy, `failed`, or `low_speech`) and 0 only when it is 1 (`exit_code()` in `cli.py`).

A transcript is public once uploaded, whatever the producer or a worker stage does to its status afterwards, except `low_speech`: `public_predicate()` in `src/docket/services/transcripts.py` is `uploaded_at IS NOT NULL AND status <> 'low_speech'`, and `get_public_transcript()`, `excerpt_for_item()`, and `search_transcripts()` all use it, so there is no list of public statuses. While a re-run is in flight the row sits at a producer status (`claimed`, `audio_fetched`, `transcribed`) with `uploaded_at` set and the old segments still in `transcript_segments`, and the public transcript page, the meeting page's transcript link, the item excerpt, and search keep serving the old transcript until `upload()` replaces the segments in one transaction. A re-run of a previously uploaded row that ends `failed` or `low_speech` does not write that status: `claim_meeting()` read the whole row before its claim and carried it on the `Claim` as `prior`, and `_end_without_upload()` in `cli.py` hands it to `restore_row()` in `db.py`, which writes every column back (`status`, `producer_host`, `claimed_at`, `stage_attempts`, `last_attempted_at`, `needs_review_reason`, `last_error`, `compare_skipped_reason`, `updated_at`, and the audio and engine columns `mark_status()` writes), so `SELECT *` before the claim and after the failure print the same row, the page is unchanged, the admin held tab does not list it, and the failure is recorded only in the producer's WARNING line `meeting N re-run ended failed (...)`, its exit code, and `producer_heartbeats.last_status`. A re-run of a never-uploaded row (`uploaded_at` NULL) writes `failed` or `low_speech` as a first run does, and such a row is never public. A producer that dies mid-run leaves the row at a producer status with the old transcript still public; the snapshot died with it and `claim_meeting()` takes none from a row resting at a producer status, so the next run of that meeting, by `claim_next()` after six hours or by `--meeting-id`, ends as a first run would, and a failure then writes `failed` with `uploaded_at` set, the one path that still produces that row; the recovery is `--meeting-id N` again.

`upload()` in `db.py` does the same thing on a first upload and a re-upload: it deletes the meeting's `transcript_segments`, upserts one `transcript_speakers` row per cluster label (a null new embedding keeps the existing one), then deletes the meeting's non-manual `transcript_speakers` rows whose `cluster_label` is absent from the new output (ghost rows a re-run with fewer clusters would otherwise leave behind; `transcript_segments.speaker_id` is the only reference to the table and those rows are already gone), then clears `council_member_id`, `display_name`, `role`, `confidence`, and `method` to NULL and `needs_review` to FALSE on the non-manual rows that remain, and only then copies the segments in. The clearing closes a window of up to a day in which the public page would show the old cluster's name on a new cluster that reuses its label; because `retry_transcript()` in `src/docket/services/discrepancies.py` keys its restart stage on `method IS NOT NULL`, the cleared rows also send a retry back to `uploaded`. Rows with `is_manual` are left alone whatever their label, so a manual assignment stays attached to whichever new cluster reuses its label; the remap that would carry it to the right voice is unbuilt (`D54`).

## What a comparison pass writes

A pass starts from two event timelines, one extracted from the transcript and one from the minutes PDF. `align()` in `src/docket/ai/transcripts/aligner.py` pairs them deterministically into candidates: a transcript event with no minutes counterpart (`omission`), a minutes event with no transcript counterpart (`addition`), a pair whose details differ (`wording`, `speaker`, or `vote_outcome`), or a pair out of order (`sequence`). Every candidate except the `vote_outcome` ones goes to a language model called the judge, which keeps or dismisses each one; the full texts go to a second model call called the hunter, which reports what the aligner missed. `build_rows()` in `compare.py` turns candidates, judge verdicts, and hunter findings into rows of `minutes_discrepancies`, with every quoted excerpt verified by the guard against the text the model saw (a quote the guard cannot find keeps the row with `citation_match = 'missing'`), and `write_rows()` hands them to `reconcile()`.

A row's `review_state` is one of `proposed`, `approved`, `rejected`, `dismissed_by_model`, or `stale`. `src/docket/services/discrepancies.py` owns the human transitions: `approve()` moves a `proposed` row to `approved` and refuses a row whose `citation_match` is `missing`. The public meeting page shows `approved` rows only (`list_public_for_meeting()` and its two siblings in the same file).

## Which categories a model may assign

The six categories are `vote_outcome`, `omission`, `addition`, `wording`, `sequence`, and `speaker` (a CHECK constraint in `src/docket/migrations/035_transcripts.py`). `vote_outcome` is written only by the deterministic tally check, the first loop of `build_rows()`: a candidate the aligner marked `vote_outcome` becomes a row with `severity = 'material'` and `origin = 'aligner'`, with no model involved. A judge verdict or a hunter finding that names `vote_outcome` is written as `wording`, and the remap is logged at INFO as `remapped a model-assigned vote_outcome to wording`; `model_category()` in `compare.py` does this on both paths before the row's category is used for anything else, so a remapped hunter row folds into an aligner `wording` row on the same anchor like any other wording row (issue #114). The fold-in in `build_rows()` tests the hunter's original category as well as the remapped one against the aligner rows already built, so a hunter restating the deterministic tally mismatch on the same transcript event folds into the `vote_outcome` row instead of becoming a second `wording` row. The judge and hunter prompts in `src/docket/ai/transcripts/prompts.py` and the `Literal` types in `schemas.py` still list `vote_outcome`; removing it is debt `D50` in `docs/DEBT.md`, paid with the next `COMPARE_PROMPT_VERSION` bump.

## How a vote result is read

`canonical_result()` in `aligner.py` reads a result string from either side and returns no outcome, `fail`, or `pass`, in that order of checks. A result that names a continuation (`carried over`, `carried forward`, `tabled`, `withdrawn`, `postponed`, `continued`, `deferred`, `no action`) is no outcome. Otherwise the negation clause (`not adopted`, `not approved`, `not carried`, `not granted`) and the fail stems (`fail`, `deny`/`denies`/`denied`/`denial`, `defeat`, `reject`, `lost`) read `fail`; fail is checked before pass, so "motion to deny approved", "motion to deny carried", and "the council approves the denial" all read `fail`, and the Birmingham denial "the motion to approve NOT adopted and the above resolution adopted" reads `fail`. Otherwise the pass words (`adopted`, `approve`/`approved`, `pass`/`passes`/`passed`, `carried`, `granted`, `prevail`, `sustained`) read `pass`. Anything else is no outcome. `_results_differ()` reports a vote-outcome mismatch only when both sides name an outcome and the outcomes differ, so a side that reads no outcome skips the comparison rather than guessing (issue #116). "approved" against "adopted" is the same outcome and is at most a `wording` finding.

## Re-comparison

The comparison runs again on three paths: the weekly `sweep_minutes_changes()` in `src/docket/ai/transcripts/pipeline.py`, when a compared meeting's minutes PDF hash changed; `reset_for_versions()` in the same file, after a `COMPARE_PROMPT_VERSION` bump; and `run_for_meeting()`, which `scripts/transcript_recompare.py` drives by hand. What a pass does to rows a human already reviewed is the policy in `reconcile()`, keyed on one column the claim carries, `transcripts.compare_skipped_reason`: `'minutes_changed'` is written only by the weekly sweep and the script with `--minutes-changed`; `'transcript_changed'` is written by the `EVENTS_PROMPT_VERSION` branch of `reset_for_versions()` and the script with `--transcript-changed`, because re-extracting the transcript side deletes the events every row is anchored to. `run_compare_stage` derives `minutes_changed` and `transcript_changed` from that column and, when the first is set, also re-extracts the minutes side before comparing. `advance()` in `pipeline.py` leaves the column alone when a stage before compare reports no reason, so the marker survives the events stage's own status write; two writers still discard it, `retry_transcript()` in `src/docket/services/discrepancies.py` (NULL) and an events stage whose minutes check fails (its own reason), so a transcript retried from the held tab after either runs an unchanged-input pass (`D52` in `docs/DEBT.md`).

Every pass first deletes the meeting's `dismissed_by_model` rows; the model's old dismissals are replaced wholesale. A pass with `minutes_changed` or `transcript_changed` set then sets every `approved` and `proposed` row to `stale`, recording the old state in `previous_review_state`; any other pass stales only `proposed` rows, because on unchanged input the only thing that can make a finding vanish is the model deciding differently, and a human's approval outranks that. Each new row is then matched against the meeting's `stale`, `rejected`, and `approved` rows by `match_stale()`: same category and the same `transcript_event_id`, or for rows with no transcript event, same category and agenda item with a minutes-excerpt similarity of at least 0.8. A matched row is updated in place and keeps its id. On a `transcript_changed` pass the re-extraction has already set every row's `transcript_event_id` to NULL (the foreign key is `ON DELETE SET NULL`), and `match_stale()` matches an anchored new row only on a shared anchor, so a returning anchored finding is inserted as a new `proposed` row beside the stale approval and only unanchored `addition` rows re-match by excerpt (`D51` in `docs/DEBT.md`). A matched `stale` row becomes `proposed` again with its reviewer columns cleared, and the admin queue badges it "re-review" from `previous_review_state`. A matched `rejected` row stays `rejected`, keeping `reviewed_at`, `reviewed_by`, and `review_note` while its title, description, severity, excerpts, confidence, and `prompt_version` are refreshed. A matched `approved` row is not rewritten at all: the public page says a person reviewed it, so it keeps every column the person approved, including its text, excerpts, severity, and the `prompt_version` that produced them, and the match only stops the same finding from being inserted again beside it (`reconcile()` counts it as `kept` in its return value and its log line). An unmatched new row is inserted as `proposed`; an unmatched `stale` row stays `stale`, visible under the queue's stale tab (issue #112).

`reset_for_versions()` re-queues a compared meeting for the compare stage only when it has a `proposed` or `dismissed_by_model` row below the current `COMPARE_PROMPT_VERSION`. Approved rows survive an unchanged-minutes pass at the `prompt_version` that produced their text, and rejected and stale rows are never re-stamped, so counting any of them would re-compare the meeting on every tick after a bump. The consequence: a meeting whose only live rows are approved is not re-compared by a prompt bump; the operator re-runs it with `venv/bin/python scripts/transcript_recompare.py --meeting-id N --now`, which without a flag writes `compare_skipped_reason` as NULL and keeps every approved row untouched, with `--minutes-changed` runs a minutes-changed pass, and with `--transcript-changed` runs a transcript-changed pass; the two flags are mutually exclusive.

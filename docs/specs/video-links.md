# Video links

Which links into a meeting's video open at a moment, what that moment is, which site each one opens (YouTube at a second, or the Granicus player at a chapter), what the link says about itself, how the transcriber measures where a YouTube recording sits on its Granicus clip's clock, and where an agenda item's cue point and chapter id come from. Present tense. One module builds every such link, `src/docket/services/video_links.py`; templates print the finished link and build no URL. The meeting page's untimed "Watch video" and "Watch on YouTube" links are in `docs/specs/meeting-types.md` (Provenance), and what attaches a YouTube recording to a meeting is in the same file ("The council's YouTube channel").

## The two clocks

Every time stored for a Granicus meeting (a row whose `meetings.external_id` is all digits, the clip id, and whose `video_url` is the player page `MediaPlayer.php?view_id=2&clip_id=<id>`) is on the Granicus clock, seconds from the start of the clip: an agenda item's cue point (`agenda_items.video_timestamp_seconds`), a vote read off the video (`votes.video_timestamp`), a transcript segment, and a discrepancy's `transcript_start_s`. Nothing is stored converted; a link is built from those seconds each time a page renders.

The Granicus player can jump only to a chapter: `&meta_id=<n>` appended to the player URL, where `<n>` is the id of one of the clerk's cue points. It ignores every other way of naming a time. YouTube opens at any second (`&t=<n>s`), but the council's YouTube recording of a meeting does not start when the Granicus clip does. The offset of a recording is Granicus seconds minus YouTube seconds for the same instant, so a moment at Granicus second g is at YouTube second g minus the offset. A meeting that exists only on YouTube (a `yt-` row, whose own `video_url` is the watch URL) has its times on YouTube's clock already and needs no conversion.

## Which links are timed, and each one's moment

The vote link on an agenda item's page (`/al/<slug>/items/<id>/`, the macro `vote_source_link` in `partials/_vote_result_block.html`). `query.get_vote_for_item` sets `VoteEntry.video_link` on every vote whose `video_timestamp` is not NULL (second 0 counts) and whose meeting has a `video_url`; only a vote read off the video carries a timestamp. The link stands for the item, so a vote on the item's own meeting opens the item's moment under the consent rule below. When the item has no second of its own, the link opens at the vote's own second and keeps whatever chapter id the consent rule returned, so for an item with a chapter id and no cue point YouTube opens the vote's second and Granicus the item's chapter. A vote on another meeting (the item taken up again later) opens that meeting's video at the vote's own second with no chapter, because the item's chapter id names a chapter of the item's own clip. A vote without both fields has no video link, and the macro links the minutes or the meeting page instead.

The item link on the transcript page (`/al/<slug>/meetings/<id>/transcript/`, `partials/transcript_body.html`), printed on the first turn of each agenda item. `video_links.item_links(meeting_id)` returns a link for every agenda item of a meeting that has a `video_url`, at the item's moment under the consent rule; an item with no moment gets the plain link to the meeting's video.

The link on a minutes-versus-video row, in the public block (the meeting page, the item page, and `/minutes-vs-video/`, behind the `MINUTES_VS_VIDEO_UI` flag) and in the admin discrepancy queue. `discrepancies._attach_video_links` sets `video_link` on each row that `list_queue`, `list_public_for_meeting`, `list_public_for_item`, and `list_public_recent` return. A row quotes specific words, so on YouTube it opens at the row's own `transcript_start_s`; on Granicus, which cannot seek, it opens the chapter of the row's agenda item under the consent rule, or the plain player for a row with no item. A row with no `transcript_start_s`, or on a meeting with no `video_url`, has no link. Each row carries its own meeting's recordings, so the index is correct across meetings.

The video branch of the source button (`partials/source_anchor_button.html`), for an item whose `source_anchor` is of type `video` with a truthy `timestamp_seconds`. It calls the Jinja global `video_link`, which is `video_links.link_for` itself, with the anchor's second, the item's `external_id` as the chapter id, and the anchor's URL. No page includes this partial and nothing in `src/` or `scripts/` writes `agenda_items.source_anchor`, so the branch is exercised only by `tests/unit/test_source_anchor.py`.

## The consent rule

The consent agenda is the block of routine items the council passes with one vote. The clerk reads them out in one quick run, so their cue points land seconds apart inside that reading and mean nothing singly. `video_links.consent_block` finds the reading among a meeting's consent items (`agenda_items.is_consent`) that have a cue point: it is the largest run of cue points in which neighbours are at most `CONSENT_GAP_S` (120 seconds) apart, it exists only when that run holds at least `CONSENT_MIN_ITEMS` (3) cue points, and of two runs of one size the earlier is the block. The cue points are ordered by second and then by row id. The block's chapter id is the id of the first cue point in the run that is all digits, and the run's first id when none is.

`video_links.item_moment` applies the rule. A consent item whose cue point lies inside the block (both ends included), or that has no cue point, links to the block's start and the block's chapter. Every other item links to its own cue point and its own id: a non-consent item always, and a consent item whose cue point is outside the block (one pulled off consent and discussed separately, whose `is_consent` flag comes from the printed agenda and stays true) or whose meeting has no block. A consent item with no cue point in a meeting with no block has no moment and gets the plain link.

## How a link chooses its site

`video_links.link_for` decides, from the meeting's `video_url`, the second, and the chapter id.

A meeting with no `video_url` has no link. A meeting whose own video is on YouTube links to that video at the floor of the second, or to the plain watch URL when there is no second or the second is negative. Any other address that is not a Granicus player page is returned unchanged with no site named.

For a Granicus player page, the link opens YouTube when the meeting has an attached recording (`meeting_videos`) whose measurement can be trusted for that second: the measurement's `status` is `passed`; its `method_version` is at least `MIN_METHOD_VERSION` in `video_links.py` (a floor, so a later method's measurement is used as it is; it is 2, so a measurement made by method 1, before the ends of the coverage were checked, is not used); its `granicus_clip_id` equals the meeting's current `external_id` (a recording moved to another meeting row, or a row whose event id became a clip id, is not used on the strength of an old measurement); the second lies inside the measured coverage, start included and end excluded; and the converted second, the floor of the second minus the offset, is not negative. Recordings are tried primary first and then by row id, and the first that holds the second wins. A link may therefore land a fraction of a second early and never late by rounding.

Otherwise the link opens the Granicus player: at the chapter (`meta_id`) when the chapter id is all digits, and at the plain player page when it is not or there is none. That is the result for a recording that has not been measured, was `rejected` or `failed`, was measured below the trusted version or against another clip, or does not cover the second.

A YouTube URL is always built from the video id as `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=<id>`, with `&t=<n>s` when there is a second, never by appending to a stored URL. `video_links.youtube_id` reads the id from `watch?v=<id>` on `youtube.com`, `www.`, `m.`, and `music.youtube.com` and from `youtu.be/<id>`; a YouTube address with no readable video id is treated as any other host.

## What a link says

Each link names the site it opens, and says "chapter" only when the link carries one. The vote link reads "Watch this vote on YouTube ↗" or "Watch this vote on Granicus ↗". The transcript, minutes-versus-video, and admin links print `VideoLink.phrase`: "video on YouTube ↗", "video chapter on Granicus ↗" when the Granicus link carries a chapter, "video on Granicus ↗" when it opens the plain player, and "video ↗" for any other host. The source button reads "View Source: video on YouTube at <time> →" with YouTube's own time (no " at ..." when the link carries no second), "View Source: video chapter on Granicus →", or "View Source: video on Granicus →"; on Granicus it prints no time, because the player opens the chapter's start and not the anchor's second. For any other host the button keeps its older markup, "View Source: video at <time> →" on the anchor's URL with `t=<N>`.

## The measurement table

`meeting_video_alignments` (migration 042) holds at most one row per attached recording, keyed on `meeting_video_id` and deleted with the recording: `granicus_clip_id` (the clip it was measured against), `method_version`, `status`, `reason`, `offset_s` (Granicus seconds minus YouTube seconds), `granicus_start_s` and `granicus_end_s` (the coverage, on the Granicus clock), `score`, `runner_up`, `windows`, `windows_confirmed`, and `measured_at`. The audio is not kept anywhere.

`status` is one of three. `passed`: the two recordings were compared and the match met the pass mark; a CHECK requires such a row to carry an offset and a non-empty span. `rejected`: they were compared and the match did not; `reason` is `score`, `margin`, `windows`, `conflict`, `no_window`, or `edges`, and the row is final until the method version rises. `failed`: the audio of one side could not be fetched, converted, read, or measured; `reason` is the error's class name, a colon, and its message, and the offset, span, score, and window counts are NULL.

The transcriber is the only writer, through the `transcriber` database role, which may select, insert, and update the table and not delete from it, so a re-measure overwrites the row. The only readers of `status` are `video_links.for_meetings` and the transcriber's candidate query. To have every recording measured again, raise `ALIGN_METHOD_VERSION`; to have one measured again, the database owner deletes its row. Because the site reads `passed` rows alone and a `rejected` row at the current version is not measured again, the owner can send every link back to Granicus without a deploy by setting the `passed` rows to `rejected` with a reason of their own, and restore them later, since the offsets and spans stay in place.

## The measuring pass

An ordinary transcriber run (`transcriber/transcriber/cli.py` with none of `--meeting-id`, `--dry-run`, `--cache-audio`, `--capture-inputs`, `--replay`) begins with `measure_pass` in `transcriber/transcriber/measure.py`, after the database connection is made and before the speech model is loaded, so it does not use the graphics card. It measures at most `--measure-limit` recordings (environment `TRANSCRIBER_MEASURE_LIMIT`, default 20; 0 turns it off). `--max-hours` and Ctrl-C bound the pass and the transcription loop as one run, and nothing the pass meets keeps the run from transcribing.

A candidate (`alignment_candidates` in `transcriber/transcriber/db.py`) is a YouTube recording attached to a Birmingham meeting that is not hidden, has an all-digit `external_id`, and a `MediaPlayer.php` `video_url`, and that has no measurement, or one below the current method version, or one against a clip id other than the meeting's, or a `failed` one more than 7 days old. Recordings without a usable measurement come before retried failures, then the newest meeting first, the primary recording first within a meeting. A `rejected` row at the current version is not a candidate.

The pass walks its candidates meeting by meeting. The Granicus audio is decoded from the archive's `audio.flac` when the meeting has one and downloaded otherwise, with a pause of `MEASURE_PAUSE_S` after each request to Granicus; each recording's audio is downloaded with yt-dlp in its smallest audio format; both are converted to 8 kHz mono WAV in the work folder, compared, and deleted as soon as the recording is measured. The archive is only read. Each recording's row is saved and committed on its own.

Any error other than a database error while one recording is fetched, converted, read, or measured saves a `failed` row and the pass goes on; a failure on the Granicus side saves one for each of that meeting's candidates. The pass counts failed fetches in a row, one per meeting for the Granicus side and one per recording for the YouTube side, sets the count back to zero at any `passed` or `rejected` result, and ends at `MEASURE_MAX_CONSECUTIVE_FAILURES` (3). A database error (the table is missing, or the role lacks the grant) or anything else that escapes ends the pass with one warning and the run goes on.

The pass logs, on the logger `transcriber.measure`, one line per recording, `measure meeting <id> clip <clip id> video <video id>: ` followed by `passed offset=<s>s covers=<from>-<to>s score=<s> runner_up=<s> windows=<confirmed>/<counted>`, by `rejected (<reason>)` and the same fields, or by `failed (<error>)`; `measure: 3 fetches failed in a row; stopping`, `measure: stop requested; stopping`, or `measure: max-hours reached; stopping` when it ends early; `measure: skipped (<error>)` when a database error or an unexpected one ended it; and `measure done {'passed': p, 'rejected': r, 'failed': f} remaining=<n>` whenever it reaches its end, early or not, and not after a `skipped`. How the operator reads those lines, and the command that works through the backlog, are in `transcriber/README.md`, "Measuring YouTube recordings".

## The method and the pass mark

`transcriber/transcriber/align.py` owns the method and every one of its constants; this section says what the constants mean, and where this file names a value (under Known limits) that module is where it is set. From 8 kHz audio it computes smoothed log band-energy envelopes, cross-correlates the YouTube recording against the clip, and takes the best offset, its score, and the runner-up (the best score at any other offset). It then checks the recording in windows of `WINDOW_S`, plus one window that ends where the overlap of the two recordings ends: a window that lies inside the clip is counted, and is confirmed when its best nearby match is within `AGREE_S` of the whole-recording offset. `verdict` passes a recording only when at least one window was counted (`no_window` otherwise), the score reaches `PASS_SCORE` (`score`), it exceeds the runner-up by `PASS_MARGIN` (`margin`), the confirmed share of counted windows reaches `PASS_WINDOW_SHARE` (`windows`), and no window matches strongly, at `CONFLICT_SCORE` or more, at a different offset (`conflict`, the mark of a recording that was cut or edited). Coverage is the span from the first confirmed window's start to the last confirmed window's end with each end moved inward: the span is checked from each end in pieces of `EDGE_S` seconds, each by the test a window gets, and the coverage runs from the first piece that confirms to the last, on the Granicus clock and clipped to the clip. The pieces do not enter the pass mark; a recording that meets it and has no confirming piece is rejected as `edges`. A change to the arithmetic or to a constant raises `ALIGN_METHOD_VERSION`, which is 2 since the pieces. Decision `0078` records the data the pass mark was chosen from, and decision `0081` the pieces and what they cost.

## Where a cue point and a chapter id come from

A cue point is a marker the clerk places in a Granicus recording where an agenda item begins. The player page lists them as index points, each with a time and an id, and that id is the chapter id. `GranicusAdapter.fetch_agenda_items` in `src/docket/adapters/granicus.py` reads them: an item first ingested from the player page gets the point's id as `agenda_items.external_id` and its time as `video_timestamp_seconds`.

An item ingested from the agenda PDF before the recording existed has the id `event-<n>-<item number>` and no timestamp. Once the meeting has a clip id, `_backfill_video_timestamps` in `src/docket/services/ingest.py` runs on each ingest of that meeting: when the meeting has a numbered item with no timestamp it fetches the player page once, matches index points to items by the "ITEM N" in each point's title (the first point that names a number speaks for it), and writes every change of the meeting in one statement. A NULL timestamp is filled and a stored one is never replaced. An item's `external_id` becomes the point's chapter id when the stored id is NULL or not all digits, the point's id is all digits, exactly one item of the meeting carries that item number, and no other row of the meeting holds or is being given that id; an id left alone for either of the last two reasons is logged at WARNING. The count the pass returns is added to the "Agenda items: N inserted" figure the ingest logs, so that figure includes rows the pass changed.

A meeting whose numbered items all have timestamps is never fetched by that pass, so items that were cued before the pass wrote chapter ids keep their old ids until the operator runs `scripts/correct_agenda_chapter_ids.py --city <slug>` (`correct_chapter_ids` in the same module). It visits the city's clip meetings that have a numbered, cued item whose id is NULL or not all digits, newest first, at most `--limit` (default 50), runs the same pass on each with the precheck skipped, and prints one line per meeting and a totals line; `--dry-run` computes and prints the same and writes nothing. With the precheck skipped the pass logs at WARNING every other reason a numbered item's id is left alone, one line per meeting and reason: the player page has no index points, none of them names an ITEM number, no point names the item's number, or the item's point has no all-digit id. The ingest run does not log those four. A player-page request is followed by the adapter's delay whether or not it succeeded. The rewrite is repeatable and is not reversible from the database alone, since the old id is not kept. The order the operator runs it in is in `docs/specs/operations.md`. The older `scripts/backfill_agenda_timestamps.py` fills timestamps only and never writes a chapter id.

## Known limits

Each of these is a state the code does not detect or does not handle; the debt rows named are in `docs/DEBT.md`.

A YouTube link may land up to 2 seconds (`AGREE_S`) from its moment, and a recording with a step of up to that size in it, such as a brief stream dropout, passes with the later part that far off. A recording with a larger step is rejected whole and all its links stay on Granicus (`D98`).

Coverage runs only from the first confirmed window to the last, and inside those two only from the first 60-second piece that confirms to the last, so a quiet stretch at either edge of a recording links to Granicus although the recording holds it (up to four minutes at an end on the labelled recordings), and no window is anchored to the start of the overlap, so the first minutes of a recording that starts before its clip are not covered (`D95`). A recording shorter than one window, five minutes, is rejected as `no_window` (`D97`).

Other audio at the start or the end of a recording (a cut, an edit, a holding slate, silence) is left out of the coverage by the pieces, with two limits that decision `0081` accepts: the piece that holds the boundary can confirm on its matching part, so up to 60 seconds of other audio can stay covered, and a piece of other audio confirms by accident about one time in twenty, which can leave more. A moment in such a stretch opens YouTube at the wrong second. A Granicus clip trimmed in place after it was measured keeps its clip id, so its stored offset is stale and nothing detects it (`D99`). A video removed from YouTube after it was measured still receives links (`D101`).

A consent item whose vote was read off the video long after the consent block, while the item's cue point is inside the block or missing, has a vote link that opens the reading of the consent agenda and not the later vote (`D100`).

A meeting with a numbered item that never gets a cue point has its player page fetched on every ingest run, and any id the rewrite leaves alone there is logged again each time (`D102`). The correction script matches items to index points by agenda number, which is wrong for a meeting first ingested from the player page whose items carry the adapter's fallback ids `<clip>-<position>` (`D105`), and it lists a meeting it cannot fully correct again on every run (`D106`).

The backlog command and a run stopped during the measuring still load the speech model before exiting, and the pass writes no heartbeat (`D103`). The source button treats an anchor at second 0 as untimed, and when a page hands it a `video` it builds the link from that object's `video_url` without the partial's safety check on it (`D104`). A discrepancy row's chapter is taken from whatever agenda item the row names, with no check that the item belongs to the row's meeting.

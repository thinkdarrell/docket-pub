# Votes

How a vote and its member votes are produced, what each column of `votes` and `member_votes` means, which council member a stored member name is taken to refer to, when that resolution runs, what the video OCR pipeline writes when it cannot read a vote, and how a vote read off the video is attached to an agenda item. Present tense. The schedule is the code in `src/docket/worker/scheduler.py`; this file names the tasks and their order, not their clock times.

## Two sources

A `votes` row holds one motion of one meeting: `result`, the counts `yeas`, `nays`, and `abstentions`, `source`, `confidence` (`high`, `medium`, or `low`), `needs_review` with its `review_reason`, and `raw_text`. A `member_votes` row holds one member's position on one vote: `vote_id`, `member_name` (the string the source printed, stored exactly as printed), `position`, and `council_member_id`, the `council_members` row the name refers to, or NULL when it is not known.

`minutes_text` rows come from the city's minutes PDF. `_ingest_votes` in `src/docket/services/ingest.py` runs once per meeting whose minutes have not yet been scanned, calls the adapter's `fetch_votes`, which parses the PDF with `src/docket/analysis/minutes_parser.py`, and inserts one `votes` row per motion with `result` (`passed` or `failed`, counted from the names the parser read as ayes and nays; an equal count is written as `passed`, the parser's rule for Birmingham, where the president breaks a tie; a minutes row never carries `tabled`), the three counts, and the `raw_text` of the motion, and one `member_votes` row per member with the position `yea`, `nay`, `abstain`, or `absent`, the last from the meeting's attendance block. The names the parser produces are bare surnames as the PDF prints them, curly apostrophe included (`O’Quinn` carries U+2019).

`video_ocr` rows come from the OCR pipeline over the meeting video (`src/docket/analysis/ocr/pipeline.py`) through `persist_detected_votes` in `src/docket/services/video_ocr.py`. They carry `video_timestamp`, `header_result` (what the on-screen header read), the positions `yes`, `no`, `abstain`, or `absent` under the names the OCR roster uses (`C. Woods`), and `confidence` `medium` when the vote is flagged for review and `high` when it is not. The two sources are never merged: a meeting with both has both sets of rows, each under its own `source` (decision `0005`).

Where a count is printed per member, one source counts per meeting. The member page at `/al/<slug>/council/<id>/` (`member_detail` in `src/docket/web/public.py`) prints attendance, alignment with the majority, and the roll-call total from `get_member_stats` in `src/docket/services/query.py`, and `get_member_vote_summary` counts positions the same way: both join each member vote through `briefing.chosen_source_cte` (`src/docket/services/briefing.py`, decision `0056`), the one owner of the rule the city page's counts use, reached by a function-local import because `briefing` imports `query` at module level. For each meeting the chosen source is `minutes_text` when the meeting has any vote from the minutes with `needs_review` false, otherwise `manual`, then `api`, then `video_ocr`; a vote with `needs_review` true is never counted. The rule is per meeting, not per motion: on a meeting the minutes cover, a motion only the video recorded is not counted, and which of two readings of one motion is right is not decided anywhere; `D28` in `docs/DEBT.md` is the reconciliation that stays unbuilt, and the meeting page shows both readings. The page's history (`list_member_voting_history` without `since`, and the `recent` list of `get_member_vote_summary`) is not a count: it lists every row carrying the member's id, flagged rows included, each with its source in its own column ("official minutes" or "video OCR", the meeting page's words), so a motion both sources recorded appears twice, once per source, under a line beneath the stat strip saying that the official minutes are counted where a meeting has them and video OCR otherwise, and that a flagged vote is listed below but not counted. With `since` the history too is restricted to the chosen source (`docs/specs/city-page.md`, Parameters on existing routes), and the line says instead that a flagged vote is neither listed nor counted; on the Sponsored view, which lists agenda items, the line stops at the counting rule. The Attendance card's sub-line is `1 roll call` or `<n> roll calls`. The Dissent filter, dated or not, is `query.AGAINST_OUTCOME_PREDICATE`, a no on a motion that passed or a yes on one that failed (decisions `0057` and `0066`).

## Which member a row names

The rule is `RosterIndex.resolve` in `src/docket/services/member_votes.py`, and it is the only owner of the rule. Each `council_members` row of the vote's municipality yields a set of variants, the short forms its full name can take in a source: the surname; the first initial, a dot, a space, and the surname; and when the surname contains an apostrophe or a hyphen, the surname with both stripped, the initial form of that, and the remainder after a prefix of `O'`, `Mc`, or `Mac`. A trailing generational suffix (`II`, `III`, `IV`, `Jr`, `Jr.`, `Sr`, `Sr.`) is dropped first. `Darrell O'Quinn` gives `o'quinn`, `d. o'quinn`, `oquinn`, `d. oquinn`, and `quinn`; `Clinton P. Woods` gives `woods` and `c. woods`; `Paul S. Simmons II` gives `simmons` and `p. simmons`.

Both sides are normalised before comparison: the curly apostrophes U+2019, U+2018, and U+02BC are folded to the straight one, runs of whitespace collapse to one space, the ends are trimmed, and the text is lowercased. A stored name resolves to a member when its normalised form equals one of that member's variants, the member belongs to the vote's municipality, and the member is seated on the meeting date under the half-open term window `term_start <= meeting_date < term_end` (decision `0004`): on Birmingham's transition day, 2025-10-28, the incoming member resolves and the outgoing one does not. A NULL `term_start` or `term_end` is unbounded on that side. A vote on a meeting whose `meeting_date` is NULL stays unresolved. A name that matches more than one seated member of the municipality on that date stays unresolved and is logged at WARNING as `member_votes: ambiguous name=... date=... members=[...]`; no guess is written.

The OCR path resolves at insert through its own `member_map`, built by `src/docket/analysis/ocr/rosters.py` for the meeting's seated roster and keyed by the initial-dot-surname form (`C. Woods`); the service's variant list contains that same form, so a `video_ocr` row the map missed resolves in the daily sweep below. An OCR name absent from the map is inserted with a NULL id and a WARNING line.

## When resolution runs

At insert, for every `minutes_text` row: `_ingest_votes` loads the municipality's roster index once per meeting, before its insert transaction, and stores `council_member_id` in the same INSERT as the name and position.

Daily, after the morning ingest, by the worker task `resolve_member_votes` (`src/docket/worker/tasks.py`, registered in `scheduler.py` after `ingest_all` and `video_ocr` and before `vote_matching`; by hand with `venv/bin/python -m docket.worker.scheduler --run-once resolve_member_votes`). The task calls `resolve_unresolved_member_votes`, which sweeps every `member_votes` row with a NULL `council_member_id` from any source, groups them by municipality, stored name, and meeting date, resolves each group in Python against that municipality's roster, and writes one UPDATE per (municipality, stored name, member) bucket, scoped to the bucket's dates and to rows still NULL, all in one transaction. It logs `resolve_member_votes resolved=N unresolved=M distinct_names=K` at INFO, and one `member_votes: unresolved name=... municipality_id=... rows=N` line at WARNING per unresolved name, the thirty most common, then `and N more unresolved names`. It never deletes a row, and a second run finds nothing to write.

An unresolved row means one of three things: the name is not on the roster for that meeting's date (a continuing member's prior term that the roster lacks, `D67` in `docs/DEBT.md`, or a person the roster has never held), the name is parser noise (`Georgia`, `Friday`), which is a bug in `minutes_parser.py` to fix there, or the name is ambiguous on that date. The row stays as evidence of what the source printed and the meeting page still shows it by name. The member page at `/al/<slug>/council/<id>/` counts only rows that carry that member's id, and of those only the rows of each meeting's chosen source with `needs_review` false (Two sources above); its history lists every row carrying the id, so a row joins a member's page the moment it resolves and is counted when its source is the meeting's chosen one.

## What the OCR pipeline writes when it cannot read a vote

For each vote sequence the pipeline picks a terminal frame, one whose header reads as passed, failed, or tabled, reads the header again, OCRs the three count boxes, extracts member positions, and hands the three to `_cross_verify`, which returns `needs_review` and its reason. `review_reason` is exactly one of five codes. `header_not_terminal`: the second header read did not come back as passed, failed, or tabled, so the vote has no readable result; this check runs first and outranks the others, because a vote without a result is not evidence whatever its counts say. `extraction_failed`: the count boxes and the member extractor both came up empty, so the frame was a false terminal screen (a fade or a transition). `counts_mismatch`: the summed member positions do not equal the OCR'd count boxes. `header_counts_disagree`: the header's outcome contradicts the yea and nay totals. `header_inferred_from_counts`: no terminal frame with readable counts was found, either because no header read as terminal or because the terminal frame's three counts were all empty, and the result was inferred from a frame whose counts and member positions agree.

A flagged vote is stored with `needs_review` true, its reason, and `confidence` `medium`. The `result` column of a `video_ocr` row holds `passed`, `failed`, `tabled`, or `unknown`; `unknown` is written only when the header is not terminal, so every `unknown` row carries `needs_review` true with reason `header_not_terminal`. The meeting page (`src/docket/web/templates/meeting_detail.html`) shows a "Needs review" callout with the reason under a flagged vote.

## How a video vote is attached to an agenda item

An attachment is a `vote_agenda_items` row (`vote_id`, `agenda_item_id`, `association_type`, `match_method`, `match_confidence`, `provisional`, `is_manual`, `is_active`, with `UNIQUE (vote_id, agenda_item_id)`); every reader of attachments in `src/docket/services/query.py` filters on `is_active`, and the item page at `/al/<slug>/items/<id>/` shows the prevailing active one as the item's vote. A cue point is `agenda_items.video_timestamp_seconds`, the second of the Granicus clip at which the clerk marked the item as beginning in the player (`docs/specs/video-links.md`, "Where a cue point and a chapter id come from"); a `video_ocr` vote's `video_timestamp` is on the same clock.

`match_votes_by_timestamp` in `src/docket/analysis/vote_matcher.py` attaches video votes. It runs daily from the worker task `vote_matching` (`src/docket/worker/tasks.py`, registered in `scheduler.py` after `resolve_member_votes`), which calls `match_all_unmatched` over every meeting with a vote that has no active attachment, and whenever `match_votes_for_meeting` is called for one meeting (`src/docket/services/minutes_adoption.py`, `src/docket/services/maintenance.py`). It visits only `video_ocr` votes with a `video_timestamp` and no active attachment, so a vote that already has one, manual or not, is never revisited and a second run writes nothing.

The rule is `item_index_for_vote` in the same module, over the meeting's cued items ordered by cue second and then by id. A video vote belongs to the item whose cue point is the latest at or before the vote's second, except that a vote within `NEAR_CUE_S` seconds (15.0) of that cue is the close of the previous item's roll call, read off the display in the same seconds as the clerk advanced the player to the next item, and is attached to the latest item with a strictly earlier cue second instead. The step back is taken once, never repeated, so a consent vote inside a run of cue points one or two seconds apart lands on a consent item of the same run and not on the run's first item. Items that share one cue second were one click of the clerk's and are skipped together, and at a shared second the latest-cue answer is the item with the highest id. A vote before the first cue point is skipped, and a near-cue vote on the meeting's first cued item, which has no earlier item, stays on it. `compute_confidence` scores the gap from the vote's second to the cue of the item it is attached to: 1.0 under 120 seconds, 0.8 under 600, 0.5 under 1800, 0.3 beyond, halved when the vote has `needs_review` true. The row `_upsert_link` writes carries `association_type` `explicit`, `match_method` `timestamp`, `match_confidence` as scored, `provisional` false, `is_manual` false, and `is_active` true; its `ON CONFLICT` updates the association type, the method, the confidence, the excerpt, and `updated_at` only where `is_manual` is false, and never sets `is_active`, so a deactivated row for the same vote and item stays a ghost.

The matcher never changes a stored attachment. The attachments written before the near-cue rule shipped are moved by one operator step, `scripts/rematch_near_cue_votes.py --city <slug>` over `rematch_near_cue_links` in `src/docket/services/vote_rematch.py`, in the order `docs/specs/operations.md`, "Railway specifics", gives: for every active, non-manual `timestamp` attachment of the city's video votes it computes the item the rule picks today and, where that item differs and does not share the stored item's cue second, deactivates the old row and inserts the new one (or revives an inactive row for the same vote and target) in one transaction, as `explicit`, `timestamp`, `provisional` false, with the confidence rescored from the new gap; it deletes nothing, leaves a manual row alone on either side, and lists but does not move a link whose stored item is no longer even the latest cue at or before its vote (cue points changed after matching). A second run prints zero moves.

Why this rule and not the latest cue alone: GitHub issue #142. On meeting 3184 (2026-10-27) the 9–0 vote read at second 2256 closes item 3's roll call (cued at 2145; the transcript has its roll call from 2191 to 2255 and the clerk saying "Item three passes. Next item."), but item 64's cue point is also 2256, so the old rule filed the vote under item 64 with confidence 1.0 and item 3 showed no vote. Counted on production on 2026-11-07, 78 of the 237 attached video votes sat within 10 seconds of their item's cue point, 2 within 11 to 30 seconds, and 157 at 31 seconds or more, two humps with almost nothing between. The operator read 79 of the near-cue votes against their meetings' transcripts: none at or under 15 seconds named the cued item, the largest gap that named the earlier item was 11.0 seconds, and the one that named the cued item sat at 30.0, which is where 15 comes from (decision `0083`). A near-cue vote whose words name neither candidate (the clerk clicked through more than one cue point before the display was read) is still filed one item late by this rule; `D26` in `docs/DEBT.md` names them.

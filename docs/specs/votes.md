# Votes

How a vote and its member votes are produced, what each column of `votes` and `member_votes` means, what a minutes reader stores for a city whose minutes print each vote (Mobile) and what the pages print for it, which council member a stored member name is taken to refer to, when that resolution runs, what the video OCR pipeline writes when it cannot read a vote, and how a vote read off the video is attached to an agenda item. Present tense. The schedule is the code in `src/docket/worker/scheduler.py`; this file names the tasks and their order, not their clock times.

## Two sources

A `votes` row holds one motion of one meeting: `result`, the counts `yeas`, `nays`, and `abstentions`, `source`, `confidence` (`high`, `medium`, or `low`), `needs_review` with its `review_reason`, and `raw_text`. A `member_votes` row holds one member's position on one vote: `vote_id`, `member_name` (the string the source printed, stored exactly as printed), `position`, and `council_member_id`, the `council_members` row the name refers to, or NULL when it is not known.

`minutes_text` rows come from the city's minutes PDF, by one of two writers. For Birmingham, `_ingest_votes` in `src/docket/services/ingest.py` runs once per meeting whose minutes have not yet been scanned, calls the adapter's `fetch_votes`, which parses the PDF with `src/docket/analysis/minutes_parser.py`, and inserts one `votes` row per motion with `result` (`passed` or `failed`, counted from the names the parser read as ayes and nays; an equal count is written as `passed`, the parser's rule for Birmingham, where the president breaks a tie; a minutes row never carries `tabled`), the three counts, and the `raw_text` of the motion, and one `member_votes` row per member with the position `yea`, `nay`, `abstain`, or `absent`, the last from the meeting's attendance block. The names the parser produces are bare surnames as the PDF prints them, curly apostrophe included (`O’Quinn` carries U+2019). For a city whose `adapter_config` names a `minutes_reader` (Mobile, the only one), the rows are written by `src/docket/services/minutes_votes.py` from what `src/docket/analysis/mobile_minutes.py` read, and carry `minutes_reader` and five more columns; "Votes read by a minutes reader" below owns them. A row with `minutes_reader` NULL is the first writer's.

`video_ocr` rows come from the OCR pipeline over the meeting video (`src/docket/analysis/ocr/pipeline.py`) through `persist_detected_votes` in `src/docket/services/video_ocr.py`. They carry `video_timestamp`, `header_result` (what the on-screen header read), the positions `yes`, `no`, `abstain`, or `absent` under the names the OCR roster uses (`C. Woods`), and `confidence` `medium` when the vote is flagged for review and `high` when it is not. The two sources are never merged: a meeting with both has both sets of rows, each under its own `source` (decision `0005`).

Where a count is printed per member, one source counts per meeting. The member page at `/al/<slug>/council/<id>/` (`member_detail` in `src/docket/web/public.py`) prints attendance, alignment with the majority, and the roll-call total from `get_member_stats` in `src/docket/services/query.py`, and `get_member_vote_summary` counts positions the same way: both join each member vote through `briefing.chosen_source_cte` (`src/docket/services/briefing.py`, decision `0056`), the one owner of the rule the city page's counts use, reached by a function-local import because `briefing` imports `query` at module level. For each meeting the chosen source is `minutes_text` when the meeting has any vote from the minutes with `needs_review` false, otherwise `manual`, then `api`, then `video_ocr`; a vote with `needs_review` true is never counted. The rule is per meeting, not per motion: on a meeting the minutes cover, a motion only the video recorded is not counted, and which of two readings of one motion is right is not decided anywhere; `D28` in `docs/DEBT.md` is the reconciliation that stays unbuilt, and the meeting page shows both readings. The page's history (`list_member_voting_history` without `since`, and the `recent` list of `get_member_vote_summary`) is not a count: it lists every row carrying the member's id, flagged rows included, each with its source in its own column ("official minutes" or "video OCR", the meeting page's words), so a motion both sources recorded appears twice, once per source, under a line beneath the stat strip saying that the official minutes are counted where a meeting has them and video OCR otherwise, and that a flagged vote is listed below but not counted. With `since` the history too is restricted to the chosen source (`docs/specs/city-page.md`, Parameters on existing routes), and the line says instead that a flagged vote is neither listed nor counted; on the Sponsored view, which lists agenda items, the line stops at the counting rule. The Attendance card's sub-line is `1 roll call` or `<n> roll calls`. The Dissent filter, dated or not, is `query.AGAINST_OUTCOME_PREDICATE`, a no on a motion that passed or a yes on one that failed (decisions `0057` and `0066`).

## Votes read by a minutes reader

A minutes reader is a module under `src/docket/analysis/` that turns the page texts of one city's minutes file into the date its heading prints and one record per vote, opening no transaction. There is one, `mobile_minutes.py` (`READER_VERSION` 1), looked up by the name in the city's `adapter_config` key `minutes_reader` through `_READERS` in `src/docket/services/minutes_votes.py`; only Mobile's row names it. Which file is read, how it is paired to a meeting, and when, are in `docs/specs/civicclerk-ingest.md`, "Minutes files and the votes read from them". This section is what a stored row holds and what a page prints.

A vote is a line beginning `Ayes:` in the minutes, with its `Nays:` line and, where printed, its `Abstain:` line. The row stored for it has `source` `minutes_text`; `minutes_reader` `mobile`; `source_page`, the 1-based page of the PDF on which the `Ayes:` line is printed (the declaration can sit on the next page); `yeas`, `nays` and `abstentions`, the lengths of the three lists of names; `motion_text`, the motion as the minutes word it, or NULL; `declared_text`, what the presiding officer declared, in the minutes' words (`the resolution adopted`, `the waiver tabled`, `set the date for the public hearing as December 8, 2026`), or NULL when no declaration was read; `resolution_number`, the code the minutes print for the item, written `NN-NNN-YYYY` (`40-948-2026`), or NULL; `caption`, the item's caption in the minutes' capitals, or NULL; `raw_text`, the lines from the motion through the declaration; `external_id` `<the meeting's external id>-mv-<n>`, n counting from 1 in the order of the document; and `confidence` `high`, or `medium` when flagged. The member votes are `yea`, `nay`, `abstain` and `absent`, the last being the members the attendance block in force lists as absent who are in none of the three lists; names are stored as printed. A motion to adjourn, to enter executive session, to adopt the agenda, to suspend the rules or to recess takes no code, and the item pending before it keeps its code for the votes after.

`result` says whether the motion carried, and is `passed`, `failed` or `unknown`; a reader never writes `tabled` or `held over` to it. It is decided from the declaration, not from the count: with no declaration it is `unknown`; a declaration with the word `failed` is `failed`, whatever the count (an ordinance with 4 ayes and 2 nays can fail); a declaration with the word `denied` is `passed` when the motion was to deny, `failed` when the motion was not and the ayes are no more than the nays, and `unknown` otherwise; any other declaration is `passed`. So a 7 to 0 motion to deny a waiver is `passed` with `declared_text` `the ordinance denied`, and the member page's Dissent filter (`query.AGAINST_OUTCOME_PREDICATE`) lists nobody for it (decision `0125`).

A reader's `review_reason` is one of three codes, each stored with `needs_review` true and `confidence` `medium`. `declaration_not_read`: no declaration was read, and the result is `unknown`. `declaration_ambiguous`: the declaration says `denied`, the motion was not to deny, and there are more ayes than nays, so neither settles which side prevailed; the result is `unknown`. `declaration_contradicts_count`: the declaration says the motion carried with fewer ayes than nays; the result is `passed` as declared. A flagged vote is shown with the "Needs review" callout and is not counted on a member's page, as any flagged vote.

Member names are resolved at insert against the city's roster on the meeting's date ("Which member a row names" below), through one `RosterIndex` loaded once per run. A `council_members` row can carry `aliases`, further names the member answers to inside his own term, compared as any stored name is; Mobile's Scott Jones carries `Scott`, the name the roll calls of November 2021 to July 2022 print for him (decision `0127`). An alias two seated members share resolves to nobody.

A coded vote is attached to an agenda item by `link_votes_by_code` in `minutes_votes.py`, one statement for the city: a reader vote with a code and no `vote_agenda_items` row of any kind, active or not, is attached to the one item of its own meeting whose title opens with the code's first two parts (`^(\d{2}-\d{3,4})\y` on the title against `^(\d{2}-\d{3,4})-\d{4}$` on the code: `40-948 Declare the structure ...` and `40-948-2026`). Where no item or two items match, nothing is attached. The row is `association_type` `explicit`, `match_method` `minutes_code`, `match_confidence` 1.0, `provisional` false, `is_manual` false, `is_active` true. Because a vote with any link is passed over, a second run writes nothing, and a link an admin removed is not put back. That holds across a replacement of the meeting's reader votes (a file that takes the meeting, or a re-read after `READER_VERSION` is raised), which gives every vote a new id: `_replace_votes` first reads the links of the old votes that are `is_manual` or not `is_active`, and puts each, with every column as it was, on the one new vote that is the vote it was on. That vote is found by its code where one old vote and one new vote of the meeting carry the code, and otherwise by its `raw_text` where one old and one new vote carry the same text; never by its place in the file, which moves when a newer reader finds a vote an older one missed. A link whose vote cannot be found that way (two votes with the same words and no code; a vote the new reading does not hold; a file with no vote) is dropped with a warning in the log, and the vote is then as any vote with no link, which the join may attach by its code (decision `0132`). A link the join itself made is not carried; the join makes it again. It runs at the end of ingest's stage 4b on every run, so a vote stored before its item is attached on the first run after the item is stored. A reader vote with no code, or whose meeting has no such item (every Mobile meeting before 2024-06-25, which has no agenda items, `D179` in `docs/DEBT.md`), has no attachment and is shown on its meeting's page under the minutes' own caption.

No other matcher touches a reader vote. `_match_substantive`, `_match_consent_block` and `match_all_unmatched` in `src/docket/analysis/vote_matcher.py` each select `v.minutes_reader IS NULL`, so the text rules written for Birmingham's minutes never run on one and the daily `vote_matching` task does not visit a meeting whose only unattached votes are a reader's (decision `0126`).

What the pages print for a reader vote. The words come from `vote_label(result, declared_text)` and the colour from `vote_tone(result, declared_text)` in `src/docket/models/vote.py`, both Jinja globals, the one owner of what a page prints for a result: the label is the declaration where the row has one (neither NULL nor empty) and the stored result otherwise; the tone is `fail` when the result is `failed` or the declaration says `failed` or `denied`, neutral when the declaration says `tabled`, `held over`, `laid over` or `withdrawn` (whole words, any case) or the result is neither `passed` nor `failed`, and `pass` otherwise, the failed test coming first. So a carried motion to deny is red, and an item tabled is neither red nor green. An element that prints a declaration carries the class `vote-block-result--declared`, which undoes the capitals of `.vote-block-result` and `.vote-history-result` in `src/docket/web/static/layout.css`: a declaration is printed as the minutes have it.

- The meeting page (`meeting_detail.html`): the head prints the label; then the "Re:" line of the agenda item when the vote is attached to one; then, for a reader vote that has a code or a caption, one line with the code and the caption; then the motion; the count and the names; and last a link to the page of the minutes the vote is printed on. Where the meeting's minutes address is a viewer address (`is_minutes_viewer_url`; "The minutes page viewer" below) and the vote has its page, the link reads `Minutes, page <n>`, its address is docket.pub's viewer of that page, `/al/<slug>/meetings/<id>/minutes/page/<n>/`, and it opens in the same tab with `rel="nofollow"` and no arrow. Where the address passes `is_source_url_safe` and is not a viewer address (an `http` address), the link stays the direct one: `Minutes, page <n> ↗`, whose address is the meeting's `minutes_url` with `#page=<n>` (`page_link` in `vote.py`), opening in a new tab with `rel="noreferrer"`. A vote with no page, or on a meeting with no minutes link or one whose address fails `is_source_url_safe`, prints no such link. "No agenda item linked." is never printed for a reader vote.
- The item page (`partials/_vote_result_block.html`): the banner and each history row print the label and take the tone, and the vote's source link (the macro `vote_source_link`, where a video link comes first for a vote that has one) reads `Read minutes, page <n>` to the same viewer address, in the same tab with `rel="nofollow"` and no arrow, under the same condition and where the vote's record has its meeting id. An `http` address on the allowlist, or a record with no meeting id, keeps `Read minutes, page <n> ↗` to the city's file at `#page=<n>` in a new tab. A reader vote whose address fails `is_source_url_safe` prints "Vote in meeting context" and no minutes link.
- The member page (`member_detail.html`): the row prints the count and the label (`7–0 · the resolution adopted`), and where the vote has no agenda link, its caption in the place of the meeting's title, cut at 120 characters.

A vote from any other writer has `source_page` NULL and prints no page link, the stored result in capitals, and "No agenda item linked." where it has none, as before.

A re-read of a file (after `READER_VERSION` is raised) deletes the meeting's reader votes and inserts them again, so a reader vote's id, and an address ending `#vote-<id>` on a Mobile meeting page, does not survive one.

## The minutes page viewer

`GET /al/<slug>/meetings/<id>/minutes/page/<n>/` (`minutes_page` in `src/docket/web/public.py`, endpoint `public.minutes_page`, template `minutes_page.html`) shows page n of a meeting's minutes on a docket.pub page, as a picture drawn from the city's own file when a reader asks. It exists because Mobile's system answers a file request with `content-disposition: attachment` and `x-frame-options: DENY`: a link to the city's file is saved by the browser and opens at no page, and the file cannot be framed (decision `0134`, which supersedes `0128`). The route is keyed on the meeting and the page, not on a city. No session has a browser, so whether the page opens and reads well on a desk and on a phone is the operator's look still to come (`D188` in `docs/DEBT.md`).

Which address is fetched. Only the meeting's stored `minutes_url`, and only when `is_viewer_url` in `src/docket/web/source_security.py` (the Jinja global `is_minutes_viewer_url`) says yes to it: a string with no backslash and no ASCII white space or control character (a no-break space or another space outside ASCII is not refused), beginning `https://` in any letter case, with no user name or password and no port but 443, that `is_url_safe` also passes. The predicate is stricter than the allowlist because a yes lets the web process make a request, and it refuses the characters on which the allowlist's URL reader and the fetching library's disagree about the host. Nothing in the request names an address; a query string is ignored. The route executes no SQL: it reads the meeting through `docket.services.query` and hands the stored address to `docket.services.minutes_viewer.page_view`.

What each request gets.

- An unknown or inactive city, a meeting that does not exist or belongs to another city, and a hidden meeting seen by anyone but a signed-in admin: the site's 404, by the same lines as the meeting page.
- A meeting with no `minutes_url`, or one that is not a viewer address (outside the allowlist, `http`, or any other form the predicate refuses): the site's 404. A page below 1 or above `MAX_PAGE` (2,000), and a page that is not a whole number (the route's `int` converter does not match `-1` or `abc`): the site's 404. None of these sends the city a request.
- A page past the end of the file, known once the file is in hand: a 404 whose body is the viewer page, headed `Minutes, page <n>`, with the link to the city's file (the stored address with no `#page=`), the sentence `The city's file ends at page <last>; there is no page <n>. The city may have replaced the file since docket.pub read it.` and, when the file has at least one page, a `rel="nofollow"` link `Go to page <last>` (class `minutes-page-last`). No picture and no nav.
- A file that cannot be fetched (the city is down or slow, answers anything but 200, a redirect included, or sends more than `MAX_BYTES`), cannot be read as a PDF, or a request that waited longer than `WAIT_S` behind another: 200, the unavailable page, headed `Minutes, page <n>`, with the city's link at `#page=<n>` and the sentence `docket.pub could not open the city's file just now. Open it and go to page <n>.` It is a 200 and not a 502 so that the reader always gets the city's link and nothing at Cloudflare's edge replaces the page.
- Otherwise: 200 and the page.

What the page prints. The title is `Minutes, page <n> — <the meeting's label> — docket.pub`. An eyebrow line `Minutes · <the meeting's date>`; the heading `Minutes, page <n> of <count>`; `← Back to the meeting` and the meeting's label; then, in every state, the link `The city's own file (PDF) ↗` (class `minutes-city-file`, a new tab, `rel="noreferrer"`; its address is the city's own file address at `#page=<n>` from `page_link`, or with no page on the past-the-end 404), followed on a drawn page by `This is page <n> as docket.pub drew it from the city's file; the city's file is the record.` Then a nav (`aria-label="Minutes pages"`) with `← Page <n-1>` (class `minutes-page-prev`, `rel="prev nofollow"`) where there is a page before and `Page <n+1> →` (class `minutes-page-next`, `rel="next nofollow"`) where there is one after; the picture (class `minutes-page-image`), an `<img>` whose `src` is a `data:` address holding the whole picture, with its `width` and `height` and the alt text `Page <n> of the minutes of the <date> meeting, as the city published it`; and the same nav again (`aria-label="Minutes pages, below the page"`). The picture is in the one response; there is no second route that serves it. Three rules in `src/docket/web/static/layout.css` show it at the column's width whatever its own (`width: 100%`, `height: auto`), which is what fits it to a phone, and keep `Page 2 →` at the right edge on page 1. The page says it is the city's file as docket.pub drew it and makes no claim about a vote.

Headers. Every response the route builds (the drawn page, the unavailable page, the past-the-end 404) carries `X-Robots-Tag: noindex`, the page carries `<meta name="robots" content="noindex">`, and `robots.txt` disallows the path (`docs/specs/crawlers.md`). `Cache-Control` is `private, max-age=600` on a drawn page of a meeting that is not hidden and `no-store` on everything else the route builds: the unavailable page, the past-the-end 404, and any page of a hidden meeting. No response sets `Content-Disposition`.

Nothing is stored. No table, no migration, no file on disk holds the city's PDF or a drawn page. The city's file is fetched when a reader asks and held in the web process's memory: at most `CACHE_FILES` (16) files and `CACHE_BYTES` (40 MB), each for at most `CACHE_TTL_S` (an hour, counted from the fetch, so a file the city replaced is drawn from the old copy for at most an hour, and a reader's browser may then show the drawn page for the ten minutes of its `Cache-Control`, below); the least recently used file is dropped first and the one just stored never. The constants are in `src/docket/services/minutes_viewer.py`, which owns their values.

The fetch is `fetch_pdf` in `src/docket/adapters/source_file.py`, a city-neutral module that is not the CivicClerk adapter: one GET, one attempt, `https` only, no redirect followed, with `CONNECT_TIMEOUT_S`, `READ_TIMEOUT_S`, `DEADLINE_S` (3, 4 and 8 seconds; the deadline is looked at each time a chunk of `CHUNK_BYTES` is handed over, so a fetch ends within 12 when the body is sent chunked or the host falls silent, and can run past that when a body of declared length arrives slowly without a silence, `D204`) and `MAX_BYTES` (25 MB) as its bounds. It sends `User-Agent: docket.pub minutes viewer (+https://docket.pub)`, `Accept: application/pdf` and `Accept-Encoding: identity`, and refuses a body whose first 1,024 bytes do not hold `%PDF-`. The service keeps requests to one host at least `HOST_GAP_S` (a second) apart, counted from the end of the last one. Callers who ask for the same file while it is being fetched wait for that one fetch and are handed its file (single flight). A failed fetch is remembered for `FAILURE_TTL_S` (a minute) and not repeated: for the one address when the failure is about the file (a 404, a redirect, too big, not a PDF), and for every address on the host when the host did not answer (a connection error, a timeout, a status of 500 or above, the deadline). While a host is remembered as down, a file of that host already in the cache is not shown either (`D202`). The service holds no lock while it sleeps, fetches or draws.

The drawing is `render_page` in `src/docket/analysis/pdf_page.py`, with `pypdfium2` and `pillow`: the page at `WIDTH_PX` (1,200 pixels) wide, never at a scale below `MIN_SCALE` and never with a side above `MAX_SIDE_PX`, on white, saved as lossless WebP, or as PNG where the image library cannot write WebP. Both libraries are imported inside the functions, so a library that will not load gives the unavailable page and not a site that will not start. The PDF library is called under one lock, which is released before the picture is encoded. The picture has no text a screen reader can read (`D198`).

The cache, the host gap and the failure memory are each web process's own. Production runs one synchronous gunicorn worker (`docs/specs/operations.md`, Railway specifics), so there is one of each, and a fetch holds that worker while it lasts (`D197`).

What a city needs for the viewer to show its minutes: a stored minutes address that `is_viewer_url` passes (so `https`, on the allowlist, with no space in it), that answers 200 itself and not a redirect, and that answers the viewer's own User-Agent. A city that lacks one keeps the direct link (where the predicate says no) or shows the unavailable page on every viewer address (where the fetch fails); `D200` holds what to weigh then. Mobile is the only city whose votes carry a page today.

The tests that pin it: `tests/web/test_minutes_viewer.py` (the route, with the city's fetch faked, and the meeting page's link followed to a viewer page that answers), `TestViewerUrl` in `tests/unit/test_source_security.py` (the predicate), `tests/unit/test_minutes_viewer.py` (the service, some with a real second thread), `tests/unit/test_source_file.py` (the fetch), `tests/unit/test_pdf_page.py` (the drawing), `tests/web/test_mobile_minutes_votes.py` and `tests/web/test_vote_result_block.py` (the links), and `test_robots_keeps_crawlers_off_the_minutes_viewer` in `tests/web/test_crawlers.py`.

## Which member a row names

The rule is `RosterIndex.resolve` in `src/docket/services/member_votes.py`, and it is the only owner of the rule. Each `council_members` row of the vote's municipality yields a set of variants, the short forms its full name can take in a source: the surname; the first initial, a dot, a space, and the surname; and when the surname contains an apostrophe or a hyphen, the surname with both stripped, the initial form of that, and the remainder after a prefix of `O'`, `Mc`, or `Mac`. A trailing generational suffix (`II`, `III`, `IV`, `Jr`, `Jr.`, `Sr`, `Sr.`) is dropped first. `Darrell O'Quinn` gives `o'quinn`, `d. o'quinn`, `oquinn`, `d. oquinn`, and `quinn`; `Clinton P. Woods` gives `woods` and `c. woods`; `Paul S. Simmons II` gives `simmons` and `p. simmons`.

Both sides are normalised before comparison: the curly apostrophes U+2019, U+2018, and U+02BC are folded to the straight one, runs of whitespace collapse to one space, the ends are trimmed, and the text is lowercased. A stored name resolves to a member when its normalised form equals one of that member's variants, the member belongs to the vote's municipality, and the member is seated on the meeting date under the half-open term window `term_start <= meeting_date < term_end` (decision `0004`): on Birmingham's transition day, 2025-10-28, the incoming member resolves and the outgoing one does not. A NULL `term_start` or `term_end` is unbounded on that side. A vote on a meeting whose `meeting_date` is NULL stays unresolved. A name that matches more than one seated member of the municipality on that date stays unresolved and is logged at WARNING as `member_votes: ambiguous name=... date=... members=[...]`; no guess is written.

The OCR path resolves at insert through its own `member_map`, built by `src/docket/analysis/ocr/rosters.py` for the meeting's seated roster and keyed by the initial-dot-surname form (`C. Woods`); the service's variant list contains that same form, so a `video_ocr` row the map missed resolves in the daily sweep below. An OCR name absent from the map is inserted with a NULL id and a WARNING line.

## When resolution runs

At insert, for every `minutes_text` row: `_ingest_votes` loads the municipality's roster index once per meeting, before its insert transaction, and stores `council_member_id` in the same INSERT as the name and position; the minutes-reader service loads it once per run and does the same.

Daily, after the morning ingest, by the worker task `resolve_member_votes` (`src/docket/worker/tasks.py`, registered in `scheduler.py` after `ingest_all` and `video_ocr` and before `vote_matching`; by hand with `venv/bin/python -m docket.worker.scheduler --run-once resolve_member_votes`). The task calls `resolve_unresolved_member_votes`, which sweeps every `member_votes` row with a NULL `council_member_id` from any source, groups them by municipality, stored name, and meeting date, resolves each group in Python against that municipality's roster, and writes one UPDATE per (municipality, stored name, member) bucket, scoped to the bucket's dates and to rows still NULL, all in one transaction. It logs `resolve_member_votes resolved=N unresolved=M distinct_names=K` at INFO, and one `member_votes: unresolved name=... municipality_id=... rows=N` line at WARNING per unresolved name, the thirty most common, then `and N more unresolved names`. It never deletes a row, and a second run finds nothing to write.

An unresolved row means one of three things: the name is not on the roster for that meeting's date (a continuing member's prior term that the roster lacks, `D67` in `docs/DEBT.md`, or a person the roster has never held), the name is parser noise (`Georgia`, `Friday`), which is a bug in `minutes_parser.py` to fix there, or the name is ambiguous on that date. The row stays as evidence of what the source printed and the meeting page still shows it by name. The member page at `/al/<slug>/council/<id>/` counts only rows that carry that member's id, and of those only the rows of each meeting's chosen source with `needs_review` false (Two sources above); its history lists every row carrying the id, so a row joins a member's page the moment it resolves and is counted when its source is the meeting's chosen one.

## What the OCR pipeline writes when it cannot read a vote

For each vote sequence the pipeline picks a terminal frame, one whose header reads as passed, failed, or tabled, reads the header again, OCRs the three count boxes, extracts member positions, and hands the three to `_cross_verify`, which returns `needs_review` and its reason. `review_reason` is exactly one of five codes. `header_not_terminal`: the second header read did not come back as passed, failed, or tabled, so the vote has no readable result; this check runs first and outranks the others, because a vote without a result is not evidence whatever its counts say. `extraction_failed`: the count boxes and the member extractor both came up empty, so the frame was a false terminal screen (a fade or a transition). `counts_mismatch`: the summed member positions do not equal the OCR'd count boxes. `header_counts_disagree`: the header's outcome contradicts the yea and nay totals. `header_inferred_from_counts`: no terminal frame with readable counts was found, either because no header read as terminal or because the terminal frame's three counts were all empty, and the result was inferred from a frame whose counts and member positions agree.

A flagged vote is stored with `needs_review` true, its reason, and `confidence` `medium`. The `result` column of a `video_ocr` row holds `passed`, `failed`, `tabled`, or `unknown`; `unknown` is written only when the header is not terminal, so every `unknown` row carries `needs_review` true with reason `header_not_terminal`. The meeting page (`src/docket/web/templates/meeting_detail.html`) shows a "Needs review" callout with the reason under a flagged vote.

## How a video vote is attached to an agenda item

An attachment is a `vote_agenda_items` row (`vote_id`, `agenda_item_id`, `association_type`, `match_method`, `match_confidence`, `provisional`, `is_manual`, `is_active`, with `UNIQUE (vote_id, agenda_item_id)`); every reader of attachments in `src/docket/services/query.py` filters on `is_active`, and the item page at `/al/<slug>/items/<id>/` shows the prevailing active one as the item's vote. A cue point is `agenda_items.video_timestamp_seconds`, the second of the Granicus clip at which the clerk marked the item as beginning in the player (`docs/specs/video-links.md`, "Where a cue point and a chapter id come from"); a `video_ocr` vote's `video_timestamp` is on the same clock.

`match_votes_by_timestamp` in `src/docket/analysis/vote_matcher.py` attaches video votes. It runs daily from the worker task `vote_matching` (`src/docket/worker/tasks.py`, registered in `scheduler.py` after `resolve_member_votes`), which calls `match_all_unmatched` over every meeting with a vote no minutes reader wrote that has no active attachment, and whenever `match_votes_for_meeting` is called for one meeting (`src/docket/services/minutes_adoption.py`, `src/docket/services/maintenance.py`). It visits only `video_ocr` votes with a `video_timestamp` and no active attachment, so a vote that already has one, manual or not, is never revisited and a second run writes nothing.

The rule is `item_index_for_vote` in the same module, over the meeting's cued items ordered by cue second and then by id. A video vote belongs to the item whose cue point is the latest at or before the vote's second, except that a vote within `NEAR_CUE_S` seconds (15.0) of that cue is the close of the previous item's roll call, read off the display in the same seconds as the clerk advanced the player to the next item, and is attached to the latest item with a strictly earlier cue second instead. The step back is taken once, never repeated, so a consent vote inside a run of cue points one or two seconds apart lands on a consent item of the same run and not on the run's first item. Items that share one cue second were one click of the clerk's and are skipped together, and at a shared second the latest-cue answer is the item with the highest id. A vote before the first cue point is skipped, and a near-cue vote on the meeting's first cued item, which has no earlier item, stays on it. `compute_confidence` scores the gap from the vote's second to the cue of the item it is attached to: 1.0 under 120 seconds, 0.8 under 600, 0.5 under 1800, 0.3 beyond, halved when the vote has `needs_review` true. The row `_upsert_link` writes carries `association_type` `explicit`, `match_method` `timestamp`, `match_confidence` as scored, `provisional` false, `is_manual` false, and `is_active` true; its `ON CONFLICT` updates the association type, the method, the confidence, the excerpt, and `updated_at` only where `is_manual` is false, and never sets `is_active`, so a deactivated row for the same vote and item stays a ghost.

The matcher never changes a stored attachment. The attachments written before the near-cue rule shipped are moved by one operator step, `scripts/rematch_near_cue_votes.py --city <slug>` over `rematch_near_cue_links` in `src/docket/services/vote_rematch.py`, in the order `docs/specs/operations.md`, "Railway specifics", gives: for every active, non-manual `timestamp` attachment of the city's video votes it computes the item the rule picks today and, where that item differs and does not share the stored item's cue second, deactivates the old row and inserts the new one (or revives an inactive row for the same vote and target) in one transaction, as `explicit`, `timestamp`, `provisional` false, with the confidence rescored from the new gap; it deletes nothing, leaves a manual row alone on either side (a vote whose target already holds a manual inactive row therefore ends with no active attachment, and the script's line still prints it as moved, `D113` in `docs/DEBT.md`), and lists but does not move a link whose stored item is no longer even the latest cue at or before its vote (cue points changed after matching). A second run prints zero moves.

Why this rule and not the latest cue alone: GitHub issue #142. On meeting 3184 (2026-10-27) the 9–0 vote read at second 2256 closes item 3's roll call (cued at 2145; the transcript has its roll call from 2191 to 2255 and the clerk saying "Item three passes. Next item."), but item 64's cue point is also 2256, so the old rule filed the vote under item 64 with confidence 1.0 and item 3 showed no vote. Counted on production on 2026-11-07, 78 of the 237 attached video votes sat within 10 seconds of their item's cue point, 2 within 11 to 30 seconds, and 157 at 31 seconds or more, two humps with almost nothing between. The operator read 79 of the near-cue votes against their meetings' transcripts: none at or under 15 seconds named the cued item, the largest gap that named the earlier item was 11.0 seconds, and the one that named the cued item sat at 30.0, which is where 15 comes from (decision `0083`). A near-cue vote whose words name neither candidate (the clerk clicked through more than one cue point before the display was read) is still filed one item late by this rule; `D26` in `docs/DEBT.md` names them.

# Votes

How a vote and its member votes are produced, what each column of `votes` and `member_votes` means, which council member a stored member name is taken to refer to, when that resolution runs, and what the video OCR pipeline writes when it cannot read a vote. Present tense. The schedule is the code in `src/docket/worker/scheduler.py`; this file names the tasks and their order, not their clock times.

## Two sources

A `votes` row holds one motion of one meeting: `result`, the counts `yeas`, `nays`, and `abstentions`, `source`, `confidence` (`high`, `medium`, or `low`), `needs_review` with its `review_reason`, and `raw_text`. A `member_votes` row holds one member's position on one vote: `vote_id`, `member_name` (the string the source printed, stored exactly as printed), `position`, and `council_member_id`, the `council_members` row the name refers to, or NULL when it is not known.

`minutes_text` rows come from the city's minutes PDF. `_ingest_votes` in `src/docket/services/ingest.py` runs once per meeting whose minutes have not yet been scanned, calls the adapter's `fetch_votes`, which parses the PDF with `src/docket/analysis/minutes_parser.py`, and inserts one `votes` row per motion with `result` (`passed`, `failed`, or `tabled`), the three counts, and the `raw_text` of the motion, and one `member_votes` row per member with the position `yea`, `nay`, `abstain`, or `absent`, the last from the meeting's attendance block. The names the parser produces are bare surnames as the PDF prints them, curly apostrophe included (`O’Quinn` carries U+2019).

`video_ocr` rows come from the OCR pipeline over the meeting video (`src/docket/analysis/ocr/pipeline.py`) through `persist_detected_votes` in `src/docket/services/video_ocr.py`. They carry `video_timestamp`, `header_result` (what the on-screen header read), the positions `yes`, `no`, `abstain`, or `absent` under the names the OCR roster uses (`C. Woods`), and `confidence` `medium` when the vote is flagged for review and `high` when it is not. The two sources are never merged: a meeting with both has both sets of rows, each under its own `source` (decision `0005`).

## Which member a row names

The rule is `RosterIndex.resolve` in `src/docket/services/member_votes.py`, and it is the only owner of the rule. Each `council_members` row of the vote's municipality yields a set of variants, the short forms its full name can take in a source: the surname; the first initial, a dot, a space, and the surname; and when the surname contains an apostrophe or a hyphen, the surname with both stripped, the initial form of that, and the remainder after a prefix of `O'`, `Mc`, or `Mac`. A trailing generational suffix (`II`, `III`, `IV`, `Jr`, `Jr.`, `Sr`, `Sr.`) is dropped first. `Darrell O'Quinn` gives `o'quinn`, `d. o'quinn`, `oquinn`, `d. oquinn`, and `quinn`; `Clinton P. Woods` gives `woods` and `c. woods`; `Paul S. Simmons II` gives `simmons` and `p. simmons`.

Both sides are normalised before comparison: the curly apostrophes U+2019, U+2018, and U+02BC are folded to the straight one, runs of whitespace collapse to one space, the ends are trimmed, and the text is lowercased. A stored name resolves to a member when its normalised form equals one of that member's variants, the member belongs to the vote's municipality, and the member is seated on the meeting date under the half-open term window `term_start <= meeting_date < term_end` (decision `0004`): on Birmingham's transition day, 2025-10-28, the incoming member resolves and the outgoing one does not. A NULL `term_start` or `term_end` is unbounded on that side. A vote on a meeting whose `meeting_date` is NULL stays unresolved. A name that matches more than one seated member of the municipality on that date stays unresolved and is logged at WARNING as `member_votes: ambiguous name=... date=... members=[...]`; no guess is written.

The OCR path resolves at insert through its own `member_map`, built by `src/docket/analysis/ocr/rosters.py` for the meeting's seated roster and keyed by the initial-dot-surname form (`C. Woods`); the service's variant list contains that same form, so a `video_ocr` row the map missed resolves in the daily sweep below. An OCR name absent from the map is inserted with a NULL id and a WARNING line.

## When resolution runs

At insert, for every `minutes_text` row: `_ingest_votes` loads the municipality's roster index once per meeting, before its insert transaction, and stores `council_member_id` in the same INSERT as the name and position.

Daily, after the morning ingest, by the worker task `resolve_member_votes` (`src/docket/worker/tasks.py`, registered in `scheduler.py` after `ingest_all` and `video_ocr` and before `vote_matching`; by hand with `venv/bin/python -m docket.worker.scheduler --run-once resolve_member_votes`). The task calls `resolve_unresolved_member_votes`, which sweeps every `member_votes` row with a NULL `council_member_id` from any source, groups them by municipality, stored name, and meeting date, resolves each group in Python against that municipality's roster, and writes one UPDATE per (municipality, stored name, member) bucket, scoped to the bucket's dates and to rows still NULL, all in one transaction. It logs `resolve_member_votes resolved=N unresolved=M distinct_names=K` at INFO, and one `member_votes: unresolved name=... municipality_id=... rows=N` line at WARNING per unresolved name, the thirty most common, then `and N more unresolved names`. It never deletes a row, and a second run finds nothing to write.

An unresolved row means one of three things: the name is not on the roster for that meeting's date (a continuing member's prior term that the roster lacks, `D67` in `docs/DEBT.md`, or a person the roster has never held), the name is parser noise (`Georgia`, `Friday`), which is a bug in `minutes_parser.py` to fix there, or the name is ambiguous on that date. The row stays as evidence of what the source printed and the meeting page still shows it by name. The member pages at `/al/birmingham/members/<id>/` (attendance, alignment, voting history) count only rows that carry that member's id, so a row joins a member's page the moment it resolves.

## What the OCR pipeline writes when it cannot read a vote

For each vote sequence the pipeline picks a terminal frame, one whose header reads as passed, failed, or tabled, reads the header again, OCRs the three count boxes, extracts member positions, and hands the three to `_cross_verify`, which returns `needs_review` and its reason. `review_reason` is exactly one of five codes. `header_not_terminal`: the second header read did not come back as passed, failed, or tabled, so the vote has no readable result; this check runs first and outranks the others, because a vote without a result is not evidence whatever its counts say. `extraction_failed`: the count boxes and the member extractor both came up empty, so the frame was a false terminal screen (a fade or a transition). `counts_mismatch`: the summed member positions do not equal the OCR'd count boxes. `header_counts_disagree`: the header's outcome contradicts the yea and nay totals. `header_inferred_from_counts`: no terminal header could be read anywhere and the result was inferred from a frame whose counts and member positions agree.

A flagged vote is stored with `needs_review` true, its reason, and `confidence` `medium`. The `result` column holds `passed`, `failed`, `tabled`, or `unknown`; `unknown` is written only when the header is not terminal, so every `unknown` row carries `needs_review` true with reason `header_not_terminal`. The meeting page (`src/docket/web/templates/meeting_detail.html`) shows a "Needs review" callout with the reason under a flagged vote, and the vote matcher (`src/docket/analysis/vote_matcher.py`) halves the match confidence of a flagged vote when it links votes to agenda items.

## Tests that pin this

`tests/unit/test_member_votes.py` pins the normalisation, the variant list, and the term window on rows built in memory; `tests/integration/test_member_vote_resolution.py` pins the insert path and the sweep against the Birmingham roster the migrations seed, including that a noise name survives the sweep unresolved. `tests/unit/ocr/test_pipeline.py` pins the five-code decision in `_cross_verify` and that a non-terminal header is stored as `unknown` and flagged; `tests/integration/test_video_ocr_persistence.py` pins the stored triple (`unknown`, `needs_review` true, `confidence` `medium`, `header_not_terminal`).

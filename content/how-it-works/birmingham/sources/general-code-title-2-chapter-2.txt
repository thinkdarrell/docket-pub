CHAPTER 2. COUNCIL[1]

ARTICLE A. MEETINGS, RULES, PROCEDURES AND ENACTMENT OF LEGISLATION

Sec. 2-2-1. Regular meetings.

The city council shall hold regular public meetings in the city council chamber on the third floor of the city hall on Tuesday of each calendar week, commencing at 9:30 a.m. All meetings of the city council shall be held in such council chamber except when otherwise ordered or adjourned; provided that nothing in this section shall preclude conferences between the councilmembers outside the city council chamber. If such meetings fall on a legal holiday, the meeting shall be adjourned until and held on the following day at 9:30 a.m.

(Code 1964, § 2-8; Code 1980, § 2-2-1)

Sec. 2-2-2. Special or called meetings.

The president of the city council shall call special meetings of the city council whenever, in his or her opinion, the public business may require it, or on the written request of the mayor or any three councilmembers. Notice of the call of such special meetings shall be given in writing by the city clerk to the mayor, the city attorney and to each councilmember, not less than 24 hours before such special meeting shall commence or not less than 48 hours if the notice has been properly addressed and placed in the United States mail with correct postage thereon, before such meeting shall commence. Such notice shall be spread on the minutes of the meeting, unless there is entered a waiver of notice signed by all councilmembers. A special or called meeting may be held at any time without prior notice thereof, provided seven councilmembers assent thereto and a quorum is present at the holding of such meeting, and the city clerk shall have attempted to notify any absent councilmember at the office telephone number for such councilmember on file in the office of the city clerk.

(Code 1964, § 2-9; Code 1980, § 2-2-2)

State law reference(s)—Alabama Open Meetings Act, Code of Ala. 1975, § 36-25A-1 et seq.

Sec. 2-2-3. Agenda.

(a) All reports, communications, ordinances, resolutions, contract documents or other matters to be submitted to the city council shall be delivered to the city clerk by 5:00 p.m. Wednesday. Any such matter which is not delivered to the city clerk by such time shall not be voted on by council:

(1) Except by unanimous consent of the city council; or

(2) Unless approval has been obtained from the city council president for preparation of an addendum containing such matter at least 24 hours prior to the convening of the regularly scheduled council meeting.

(b) The city clerk shall immediately prepare a list of such matters according to the order of business and furnish each member of the city council, the mayor and the city attorney with a copy of such lists, together with copies of matters to be presented to the extent that such copies are available, prior to the regular, special or called council meeting and as far in advance of the meeting as time for preparation will permit.

(Code 1980, § 2-2-3; Ord. No. 90-114, 5-1-1990)

Sec. 2-2-4. Quorum.

The quorum for the transaction of any business by the city council shall be not less than five councilmembers. All measures shall be adopted by the vote of at least four councilmembers. In the absence of a quorum, the presiding officer shall, at the instance of any two members present, compel the attendance of absent members, or shall adjourn the meeting to a stated time and date to permit the establishment of a quorum.

(Code 1964, § 2-11; Code 1980, § 2-2-4)

Sec. 2-2-5. Presiding officer.

(a) The president of the city council or, in his or her absence, the president pro tempore, shall preside over regular, special and called meetings of the city council. In the absence of the president and the president pro tempore at the hour appointed for any meeting, the city clerk or his or her assistant shall call the city council to order, whereupon a temporary chairperson shall be elected by the members of the city council present. Upon the arrival of the president or the president pro tempore, the temporary chairperson shall relinquish the chairperson upon the conclusion of the business immediately before the city council.

(b) The presiding officer shall preserve strict order and decorum at all meetings of the city council. The presiding officer of the city council shall use Robert's Rules of Order, Revised, as a guide for the conduct of all meetings of the city council. The presiding officer shall state every question given before the city council, direct the clerk to call the roll and record the vote on all matters on which the recording of yeas and nays is required by law or with respect to which any councilmember requests such recording, announce the decision of the city council on all subjects and decide on questions of order. However, any councilmember may appeal any ruling to the city council, in which event a majority vote of the city council shall govern and conclusively determine the ruling appealed. The presiding officer shall vote on all questions and, on questions upon which the vote is recorded, his or her name shall be called last.

(c) The city council president shall be authorized to approve or disapprove all travel outside of the city for which reimbursement will be sought for councilmembers and staff. All requests shall be made in writing and shall state the benefits of such travel to the city. Authorization or denial of all requests shall be in writing. Any request which is denied may be appealed to the full council at a regular meeting thereof. This subsection shall not cover funds appropriated by state law to each councilmember for in-city expenses.

(d) The city council president shall, in accord with state law, recommend to the full council for its approval the yearly expense allowances of councilmembers for the purpose of attending to the business of the city within its corporate limits and during the year any adjustments or changes thereto.

(e) The city council president shall be authorized to implement policies, procedures and methods to provide security for council offices and council chambers, provided any councilmember may appeal any such policy, procedure or method to the city council, in which event a majority vote of the city council shall govern and conclusively determine such security policy, procedure or method.

(Code 1964, § 2-12; Code 1980, § 2-2-5; Ord. No. 82-74, 5-18-1982; Ord. No. 95-118, 6-27-1995)

State law reference(s)—Traveling expenses of county and municipal officers and employees generally, Code of Ala. 1975, § 36-7-1 et seq.

Sec. 2-2-6. Roll call.

Before proceeding with the business of the city council, the city clerk or the clerk's assistant shall call the roll of the members, and the names of those present shall be entered in the minutes.

(Code 1964, § 2-13; Code 1980, § 2-2-6)

Sec. 2-2-7. Order of business.

All meetings of the city council shall be open to the public. Promptly at the hour set by law on the day of each regular meeting, all councilmembers, the city clerk, city attorney, and mayor shall take their regular stations in the city council chambers, and the business of the city council shall be taken up for consideration and disposition in the following order, unless the order is suspended by unanimous consent:

(1) Roll call.

(2) Approval of minutes of the previous meetings.

(3) Communications from the mayor.

(4) Announcement of boards and agencies, expirations and board nominations.

(5) Consideration of the consent agenda.

(6) Consideration of ordinances and resolutions for final passage.

(7) Introduction and first reading of ordinances and resolutions.

(8) Old business and new business.

(9) Requests from the public.

a. All persons addressing specific agenda items that are not designated public hearings shall be granted three minutes to speak following city council presentations; and

b. Persons addressing issues of general concern shall be granted three minutes to speak immediately before old and new business. Speakers must sign the speaker's list by 9:30 a.m. and cannot address the city council at two consecutive council meetings.

(10) Presentations.

(11) Adjournment.

(Code 1980, § 2-2-7; Ord. No. 02-134, § 1, 9-17-2002; Ord. No. 08-138, 6-24-2008)

Sec. 2-2-8. Reading of minutes.

Unless a reading of the minutes of a city council meeting is requested by a member, such minutes may be approved without reading. The clerk shall furnish each councilmember with a written copy of the minutes of each meeting.

(Code 1964, § 2-15; Code 1980, § 2-2-8)

Sec. 2-2-9. Motions.

(a) No motion made at any council meeting shall be debated or put to a vote unless seconded. When seconded, the proposer shall have the floor.

(b) Each resolution or ordinance introduced at a council meeting shall be in writing and read by the clerk at the time it is introduced. Motions shall be reduced to writing when required by the presiding officer of the city council or any member of the city council.

(c) When a question is under debate, the following motions shall be in order and shall have precedence over each other in the order stated:

(1) To adjourn to a day certain.

(2) To adjourn.

(3) To take a recess.

(4) To lay on the table.

(5) To previous question.

(6) To refer to a committee.

(7) To amend.

(8) To postpone to time certain.

(9) To postpone indefinitely.

Motions 1 through 5 shall be decided without debate.

(Code 1964, § 2-16; Code 1980, § 2-2-9)

Sec. 2-2-10. Rules of debate.

(a) The presiding officer of the city council may move, second and debate from the chairperson, subject only to such limitations of debate as are by this article imposed on all councilmembers, and shall not be deprived of any of the rights and privileges of a councilmember by reason of his or her acting as the presiding officer.

(b) Any councilmember desiring to speak shall address the chairperson and upon recommendation by the presiding officer shall speak to the question under debate, avoiding any references of a personal nature and the use of any indecorous language.

(c) A councilmember or official, once recognized, shall not be interrupted only if the councilmember desires to seek a point of order from the chair. If a point of order is used, the person who is speaking shall cease until the question of order is determined.

(d) No person shall speak more than twice on any matter before the city council except by unanimous consent, and then not until every councilmember desiring to speak has had an opportunity to do so. No person shall speak more than six minutes on any subject, except by permission granted by a majority vote. However, the councilmember moving the adoption shall have the privilege of closing debate and shall not be limited on answering questions pertinent to the matter proposed.

(Code 1964, § 2-17; Code 1980, § 2-2-10; Ord. No. 02-135, § 1, 9-17-2002)

Sec. 2-2-11. Communication with city council.

(a) Interested parties and their authorized representatives may orally address the city council on any subject by signing their legal name to a speaker's request form prior to the start of the city council meeting. Such person shall be allowed to speak for three minutes provided that the total time for all such oral presentations shall not exceed 30 minutes unless further time is granted by the city council. No person shall be allowed to address the city council at two consecutive meetings unless granted by the city council.

(b) Where required by law, or by unanimous consent of the city council, public hearings on matters before the city council shall be held by the city council at which taxpayers or residents of the city or their authorized legal representatives shall be permitted to present oral statements for or against the matter made a subject of the hearing. Requests to appear at such a public hearing may be filed in writing with the city clerk, who shall refer the same to the president of the city council prior to the hearing. Persons making these requests in writing shall, together with witnesses requested to appear by the city council, be heard prior to other persons who appear at the hearing.

(c) Taxpayers or residents of the city or their authorized legal representatives shall be permitted to orally address any committee of the city council on any matter referred to that committee. Requests to appear before a committee of the city council shall be filed with the city clerk, who shall refer the same to the chairperson of the committee. The chairperson of the committee may call a meeting of the committee to hear the matters presented, or shall bring the matter before the city council to set a time and place for a public hearing.

(d) Each person addressing any public hearing shall, when called, step up in front of the rail, give his or her name and address in an audible tone of voice for the records, and, unless further time is granted by the city council, limit his or her address to three minutes. All remarks shall be addressed to the city council or the committee as a body and not to any member thereof. No person other than a councilmember or the person having the floor shall be permitted to enter into any discussion, either directly or through a member of the city council, without the permission of the presiding officer. All questions to councilmembers shall be directed through the presiding officer.

(Code 1964, § 2-18; Code 1980, § 2-2-11; Ord. No. 91-82, 4-23-1991; Ord. No. 94-89, § 1, 5-10-1994)

Sec. 2-2-12. Decorum.

(a) While a council is in session, the members shall preserve order and decorum and a member shall neither, by conversation or otherwise, delay or interrupt the proceedings or the peace of the city council nor disturb any member while speaking or refuse to obey the orders of the city council or its presiding officer, except as otherwise herein provided.

(b) Any person not a member of the city council, making personal, impertinent or slanderous remarks or who shall become boisterous while the city council is in session, shall be forthwith, by the presiding officer, barred from further audience before the city council, unless permission to continue is granted by a majority vote of the city council.

(c) The chief of police or those members of the police department as the chief may designate shall be sergeant-at-arms of the city council meetings. The chief or members of the department shall carry out all orders and instructions given by the presiding officer for the purpose of maintaining order and decorum at the city council meeting. Upon instructions of the presiding officer, it shall be the duty of the sergeant-at-arms or any of them present to place any person who violates the order and decorum of the meeting under arrest and cause him or her to be prosecuted upon complaint to be signed by the presiding officer.

(Code 1964, § 2-19; Code 1980, § 2-2-12)

Sec. 2-2-13. Persons authorized to be within the rail.

No person, except city officials and their representatives, shall be permitted within the rail in front of the city council chamber without the express consent of the city council.

(Code 1964, § 2-20; Code 1980, § 2-2-13; Ord. No. 91-83, 4-25-1991)

Sec. 2-2-14. Committees.

(a) All standing and special committees shall be appointed by the president of the city council, subject to approval by the city council. A quorum of two members of any committee except for the committee of the city council as a whole shall be required in order to conduct business. The chairperson of each committee shall exercise complete discretion in allowing public participation in committee meetings.

(b) No action of any committee including the committee of the city council as a whole shall be considered final without a formal vote of the full council at a regular council meeting.

(c) A record shall be made of all committee proceedings which shall be made available to the public and other councilmembers.

(d) All committee meetings shall be scheduled and recorded in the city council meeting record book at least 12 hours prior to each meeting.

(Code 1980, § 2-2-14; Ord. No. 82-75, 5-18-1982)

Sec. 2-2-15. Ordinances, resolutions, motions and contracts.

(a) Any ordinance or resolution shall be prepared by the city attorney upon the written request of the president of the city council. Any ordinance or resolution not prepared by the city attorney shall be submitted to him or her for review as to form and legality prior to the entertainment by the city council of any motion to adopt that ordinance or resolution.

(b) All ordinances, resolutions and contract documents shall, upon introduction, be submitted to the mayor for his or her administrative recommendation with reference thereto.

(c) Ordinances, resolutions and other matters or subjects requiring action by the city council may be submitted by a councilmember, the mayor or city attorney. Any councilmember may assume sponsorship thereof by moving that such ordinances, resolutions, matters or subjects to be adopted; otherwise, they shall not be considered.

(1) At least four copies of any ordinance or resolution to be submitted or introduced at any meeting of the city council shall be filed with the city clerk prior to the meeting. Copies shall be distributed to each councilmember and to the mayor and to department heads and staff as directed by the mayor. Such copies may be provided by the clerk to the councilmembers, mayor and staff in electronic format. After the meeting, the city clerk shall prepare additional copies, if such be necessary, to supply to interested citizens or taxpayers requesting copies at his or her office, at a reasonable charge for such copies.

(2) Unless waived by unanimous consent, every ordinance or resolution requiring council approval shall be submitted to a committee of the council prior to submission of the item to the council. The committee shall report thereon at the next regular meeting of the council after the committee meets, unless further time is granted by majority vote of the council. When the committee to which an ordinance or resolution has been referred shall report such ordinance or resolution to the council at a subsequent meeting, the council may consider the ordinance or resolution as reported by the committee, including any amendments or substitutions proposed by the committee.

(3) No ordinance shall relate to more than one subject, which shall be expressed clearly and briefly in its title; provided a single ordinance may amend one or more sections of the code which relate to the same general subject matter as contained in a single title, chapter or article thereof.

(4) No ordinance or code section or any portion thereof shall be amended or repealed unless the new ordinance contains the title or designation of the ordinance or code section amended or repealed. When practicable, all ordinances shall be introduced as amendments to existing ordinances or code sections. Prior to adoption, the presiding officer shall refer any ordinance or resolution to the committee of the whole. An ordinance, after reference and report thereon, may be referred at any time prior to its final passage.

(5) In the event the council votes to refer an ordinance or resolution to committee, the motion to refer the matter to committee shall include a reasonable specified time not to exceed 21 calendar days or the next regularly scheduled council meeting following that committee meeting, whichever is less, unless otherwise provided by majority vote of the city council or a time period required by law, at which time the committee shall report the matter to the council for final action. Any matter which has been referred to committee shall remain in committee until the committee reports the matter out of committee at the specified time, provided, however, that council, by the affirmative vote of a majority of the council members voting, but in no event less than four affirmative votes, may discharge the committee from further consideration of the matter referred, in which event the matter shall return to the council for final action at the next regularly scheduled meeting.

(6) In the event the council votes to postpone consideration of an ordinance or resolution, such ordinance or resolution will be included on the agenda for the council meeting on the date to which the matter was postponed without having to be re-submitted in accordance with the procedure for submission set forth in this section.

(7) Unless brought before the council pursuant to a duly made motion for reconsideration, any ordinance or resolution which fails to receive approval of the council at the meeting in it is voted upon, will be required to comply with the procedure for submission of an original ordinance or resolution set forth in this section.

(Code 1980, § 2-2-15; Ord. No. 90-118, 5-1-1990; Ord. No. 16-237 , § 1, 1-3-2017)

State law reference(s)—No ordinance shall be adopted by council at the same meeting at which it is introduced, unless unanimous consent is given by those present, Code of Ala. 1975, § 11-45-2.

Sec. 2-2-16. Incontestability of duly adopted legislation.

No ordinance, resolution or action duly adopted or taken by the city council shall be held to be invalid because of failure of the city council to comply with or abide by any one or more of the provisions of this article, if the ordinance, resolution or action would otherwise be valid under the laws of this state but for the provision of this article alleged to have been violated or ignored.

(Code 1964, § 2-23; Code 1980, § 2-2-16)

Sec. 2-2-17. Written reports.

(a) All written reports to the city council shall be filed with the city clerk and entered in the minutes of the meeting at which the report was submitted.

(b) All precouncil proceedings shall be recorded and said record shall be retained and made available to the public for a period of no less than one year.

(Code 1964, § 2-24; Code 1980, § 2-2-17; Ord. No. 82-76, 5-18-1982)

Sec. 2-2-18. Terms of members of governing body of board or agency.

(a) No person appointed by the city council to the governing body of a board or agency shall be allowed to serve more than two consecutive full terms unless the city council, in extreme circumstances, expressly overrules this policy or unless state or federal law requires otherwise.

(b) Councilors are directed to solicit and encourage the submission of resumes of persons qualified and willing to serve on the governing body of a board or agency and to organize and preserve such resumes for council's use when a vacancy occurs.

(c) Councilors are directed to develop an orientation program for prospective board and agency appointees, including written materials on the specific duties of members of active boards and agencies and a general information pamphlet for all.

(d) The limit of two consecutive full terms shall not apply to members of the following listed boards and agencies:

(1) Any medical clinic board of the city.

(2) Any special care facility financing authority.

(3) Public building authority.

(4) Red Mountain Museum.

(5) Ruffner Mountain Nature Center.

(6) Southern Museum of Flight.

(7) Museum of Art.

(8) Sloss Furnace Board.

(9) Public park and recreation board of the city.

(10) Design review committee.

(11) Jefferson County Historical Commission.

(12) Electrical examining board of competency and appeals.

(13) Elevator board of appeals.

(14) Gasfitters examining board of competency and appeals.

(15) Plumbers examining board of competency and appeals.

(Code 1980, § 2-2-18; Ord. No. 90-214a, §§ 1—3, 8-21-1990; Ord. No. 90-273, §§ 1—4, 11-6-1990)

Secs. 2-2-19—2-2-30. Reserved.

ARTICLE B. COUNCIL ADMINISTRATOR

Sec. 2-2-31. Office created.

(a) There is hereby created and established an office of the city council, the staff of which shall be supervised by an officer known as the city council administrator.

(b) The city council administrator shall be appointed by the city council and shall serve at the pleasure of the city council under the authority of Act No. 495, 1973. The city council administrator shall be paid such compensation as the city council shall from time to time establish by resolution under the authority of such section.

(Code 1964, § 2-24.1; Code 1980, § 2-2-31)

Sec. 2-2-32. Duties.

The duties of council administrator shall be under the direction of the city council and the president of the city council. The city council administrator shall, among other things:

(1) Be on call of the city council to attend committee meetings and take or cause to be taken the minutes of these meetings;

(2) Supervise the preparation of minutes and reports of council committees;

(3) Supervise the personnel assigned by the mayor or employed by the city council, under the authority of Act No. 495, 1973, in the performance of their duties;

(4) Evaluate the performance of all council staff members and make recommendations to the city council of any action to be taken with regard to council staff;

(5) Answer correspondence for members of the city council related to their office when the city council administrator has been authorized to do so by the individual councilmembers;

(6) Gather information and prepare reports for committee chairpersons;

(7) Keep councilmembers apprised of all important matters received by him or her for the city council from the public, the mayor or department heads of the city; and

(8) Perform any other duties related to the work of the city council as the president of the city council may assign.

(Code 1964, § 2-24.2; Code 1980, § 2-2-32; Ord. No. 82-77, 5-18-1982)

Secs. 2-2-33—2-2-40. Reserved.

ARTICLE C. FRANCHISES[2]

Sec. 2-2-41. Effective date; advance publication at expense of grantee.

No franchise to any railroad, water company, power company, electric company or to any company of like kind or character whose business contemplates a use of the public streets of the city, or is charged with a public interest, shall take effect and be enforced until 30 days after the final enactment of the same by the city council and publication of the same in full once a week for three consecutive weeks in some daily newspaper published in the city, which publication shall be at the expense of the persons applying for the grant.

All these grants shall be subject to the right of the legally qualified voters to object by petition, as provided by law.

(Code 1964, § 21-1; Code 1980, § 2-2-41)

Sec. 2-2-42. Franchise to be accepted in writing; prepayment of cost of publication.

Before any ordinance granting a franchise shall be published, the terms thereof must be accepted in writing by the applicant therefor, which acceptance shall be filed in the office of the city clerk; and the applicant shall, upon filing the acceptance, pay to the director of finance the cost of making the publication.

(Code 1964, § 21-2; Code 1980, § 2-2-42)

Sec. 2-2-43. Amendments.

All material amendments to franchises shall be subject to the same conditions as are required for the original grant.

(Code 1964, § 21-3; Code 1980, § 2-2-43)

Sec. 2-2-44. Conditions of grant.

No franchise will be granted to any person to construct and operate any waterworks, light, heating or power plant in the city except upon the payment by the applicant for the franchise into the city treasury of that sum as the city council may determine in each particular case, but, in lieu of the stated sum, the city council may require the annual payment into the city treasury of a percentage of the gross receipts arising from the operation of that utility. Permission shall not be granted to any railroad company to lay tracks on any of the streets of the city unless such company shall have complied with all the requirements of the city as to maintaining lights at crossings.

(Code 1964, § 21-4; Code 1980, § 2-2-44)

Secs. 2-2-45—2-2-50. Reserved.

ARTICLE D. SINGLE-MEMBER COUNCIL DISTRICTS

Sec. 2-2-51. Election from districts.

It is hereby established and declared that the city shall change the manner in which its nine councilmembers are elected from the city at-large election system provided for in Act No. 452 (1955), as amended (hereinafter referred to as the "Mayor-Council Act"), to the election of its councilmembers from nine single-member districts, as authorized by Code of Ala. § 11-43-63 (1988 supp.) (hereinafter referred to as Code of Ala. 1975, § 11-43-63).

(Code 1980, § 2-2-51; Ord. No. 89-46, § 1, 3-28-1989)

Sec. 2-2-52. Method of election.

The city shall elect all nine councilmembers at its regular general municipal election commencing on the fourth Tuesday in August. Each candidate for district councilmember who receives a majority of the total votes cast in the candidate's council district in the general election shall be elected for that district. In the event that any one candidate should fail to receive such a majority of the total votes from that district in the first election, then for such districts a second (or runoff) election shall be held on the sixth Tuesday thereafter in the same mode and manner and under the same rules and regulations as the first election. In the second election, there shall be two candidates for each council district seat to be filled in such election; and these candidates shall be the ones who received the highest number of votes in said council district, but who were not elected in the first election. The candidate receiving the higher number of votes cast in each district for which a second election is held shall be elected, so that in the first and second elections all nine councilmembers shall be elected. No voter shall vote in either election provided for in this section more than one time, nor shall any voter cast a vote in either election for more than one candidate for council. Only voters residing within a council district shall be entitled to vote for candidates seeking election as their district councilmember.

(Code 1980, § 2-2-52; Ord. No. 89-46, § 2, 3-28-1989)

Editor's note(s)—The regular general municipal election dates appearing in § 2-2-52Editor's note(s)— were changed by § 1 of Ordinance No. 09-36, adopted February 24, 2009, to the fourth Tuesday in August 2009 and quadrennially thereafter, and, when necessary, any second or runoff election would be held on the sixth Tuesday next thereafter following the regular election. The ordinance further provided that the term of office for the councilmembers so elected would commence on the fourth Tuesday in November following their election. Section 2 of Ordinance No. 09-36 changed the regular election for mayor in 2011 to the fourth Tuesday in August and quadrennially thereafter, and, when necessary, any second or runoff election would be held on the sixth Tuesday next thereafter following the regular election. Section 2 further provided that the term of the mayor so elected would commence on or before the fourth Tuesday of November of the year of such election.

Sec. 2-2-53. Membership; term of office.

The city council shall consist of nine members who shall be known as district councilmembers. The term of office for the nine district councilmembers elected from the single-member districts shall commence on the fourth Tuesday in October following their election. District councilmembers elected under the provisions of this article shall hold office for a term of four years and until their successors are elected and qualified. Subsequent elections of councilmembers shall be held at the same time and in the same manner as provided for in section 2-2-52.

(Code 1980, § 2-2-53; Ord. No. 89-46, § 3, 3-28-1989)

Editor's note(s)—The date for commencing the term of new councilmembers appearing in section 2-2-53Editor's note(s)— was changed by § 2 of Ala. Act No. 2012-223 to the fourth Tuesday in October.

Sec. 2-2-54. Effect of article on Mayor-Council Act.

No provision of this article shall have the purpose or effect of increasing or decreasing the number of councilmembers established by the Mayor-Council Act. Code of Ala. 1975, § 11-43-63 specifically authorizes the city to divide itself into single-member districts, not to exceed nine in number.

(Code 1980, § 2-2-54; Ord. No. 89-46, § 4, 3-28-1989)

Sec. 2-2-55. Effect of article on office of mayor.

No provision of this article shall have the purpose or effect of changing the time or manner for election to the office of mayor of the city, as specified in the Mayor-Council Act.

(Code 1980, § 2-2-55; Ord. No. 89-46, § 5, 3-28-1989)

Sec. 2-2-56. Applicability of state law.

Elections shall, except as otherwise provided herein, be governed by the general provisions of Alabama law relating to municipal elections for Class 1 cities.

(Code 1980, § 2-2-56; Ord. No. 89-46, § 6, 3-28-1989)

Sec. 2-2-57. Statement of candidacy.

Any person seeking to become a candidate in any election for the office of district councilmember may become such candidate by filing in the Office of the Judge of Probate of Jefferson County, Birmingham Division, a statement of such candidacy and an affidavit taken and certified by such judge of probate or by a notary public that such person is duly qualified to hold the office for which the person desires to be a candidate. Such statement shall be filed at least 45 days before the first day set for the general election and shall be in substantially the following form:

"State of Alabama, ___________ County. I, the undersigned, being first duly sworn, depose and say that I am a citizen of the City of Birmingham, in said State and County, and reside at ___________, in said City of Birmingham, that I desire to become a candidate for the office of district councilmember for District Number ________, in said City at the election for said office to be held on the ________ day of August next and that I am duly qualified to hold said office if elected thereto and I hereby request that my name be printed upon the official ballot at said election. Signed ___________, on this ___ day of ___________, ________, and filed in this office for record on said day. ___________, Judge of Probate."

Said statement shall be accompanied by a qualifying fee in the amount of $50.00, which fee shall be paid over by the judge of probate to the general fund of the City of Birmingham. At every such election all ballots to be used by voters shall be printed and prepared by the election commission or body or official charged by law with the duty of conducting elections, and at the expense of the City of Birmingham, and shall contain the names of all candidates, grouped by district, directly underneath the words "For Members of Council." No name shall appear upon said ballot as a candidate for election except the names of such persons who have become candidates according to provisions as above set forth; no ballot shall be used at any such election except the official ballot prepared by the election commission or other body or official charged by law with the duty of conducting elections, except that the names of candidates may be suitably placed on voting machines if such machines are used to conduct such election. Sec. 2-2-58. Residence within district.

Except as provided in section 2-2-62, each district councilmember shall reside within the boundaries of the city council district for which the person seeks election, and shall reside within the district during such person's term of office on the city council. Further, each candidate for election to a place on the city council shall have resided within the district from which the candidate seeks election for a period 90 days immediately preceding the date of the first election of the general municipal election. In addition, councilmembers shall meet the eligibility requirements set forth in section 3.04 of the Mayor-Council Act, which said requirements are incorporated herein as a part of this article. If a councilmember shall cease to possess any of the eligibility requirements enumerated in this section or section 3.04 of the Mayor-Council Act, the councilmember's office shall immediately become vacant.

(Code 1980, § 2-2-58; Ord. No. 89-46, § 8, 3-28-1989)

Sec. 2-2-59. Council president, president pro tem.

The city council shall elect an officer of the city who shall have the title of president of the city council and shall preside at meetings of the city council. The city council shall also elect a president pro tem, who shall act as president of the city council during the absence or disability of the president. The term of office of the president and the president pro tem shall be until the councilmembers shall qualify following the next succeeding quadrennial election. If a vacancy shall occur in the office of president of the city council, the city council shall elect a successor in said office for the completion of the unexpired term. Both the president of the city council and the president pro tem shall be elected from among the councilmembers.

(Code 1980, § 2-2-59; Ord. No. 89-46, § 9, 3-28-1989)

Sec. 2-2-61. Districts.

(a) There shall be established nine council districts to be designated respectively as District Number 1, District Number 2, District Number 3, District Number 4, District Number 5, District Number 6, District Number 7, District Number 8 and District Number 9, which districts shall have as nearly as is reasonable, the same population. The designation and boundaries of the initial and subsequent council districts, a districting map depicting the nine single-member council district boundaries for each said district, district subsections and voting places for voter convenience shall be specifically described and maintained on file in the office of the city clerk and shall be available for inspection by the public at all reasonable times. A certified copy of any ordinance designating or amending boundaries of council districts, district subsections or voting places and any maps depicting such shall be filed with the judges of probate of any county which contains a portion of the corporate limits of the city.

(b) In the event there is a conflict in the city council district boundary lines set forth in the written description of district boundaries and the boundary lines as depicted on the districting map, the boundary lines depicted on the map shall govern.

Sec. 2-2-62. Reapportionment of districts.

Whenever there shall be a change in population in any of the nine districts heretofore established, as evidenced by a federal census of population published following the last federal census of population subsequent to the adoption of this article, there shall be a reapportionment of the city council districts in the manner hereinafter provided:

(1) The president of the city council or any member thereof shall, within six months after the publication of the last federal census of population for the city, file with the city clerk a proposed ordinance containing a recommended plan for reapportionment of the city council district boundaries to comply with the following specifications:

a. Each district shall be formed of contiguous and, to the extent reasonably possible, compact territory, and its boundary lines shall be the center lines of streets or other well-defined boundaries.

b. Each district shall contain as nearly as is reasonable the same population.

The proposed ordinance shall include a map and description of the districts recommended.

(2) The city council shall consider the redistricting plan or plans filed with the city clerk as follows:

a. The order of consideration of any plan shall be in the order of said filing with the city clerk.

b. After a proposed ordinance is filed with the city clerk and prior to its consideration for adoption, the city clerk shall place a resolution on the city council agenda within ten days which shall set a date for public hearing by the city council for consideration of such ordinance, such hearing to be set no less than 28 days nor more than 42 days from the date of passage of such resolution, and, which shall also provide that notice that a redistricting ordinance shall be considered shall be published once a week in a newspaper of general circulation in the city for the three successive weeks prior to the scheduled hearing, setting out a general description of the subject of the hearing, the date and time of the hearing and that a copy of the ordinance will be available for public inspection in the office of the city clerk during normal business hours.

c. No such ordinance shall become effective until after a public hearing in relation thereto, at which citizens shall have an opportunity to be heard. The first of such proposed ordinances which receives five affirmative votes of the city council shall be deemed the duly enacted redistricting ordinance.

(3) Such redistricting ordinance shall not apply to any regular or special municipal election held within six months after its becoming effective or until necessary approval of preclearance has been obtained pursuant to the Voting Rights Act of 1965, as amended, and the applicable procedures contained in part 51 of the Code of Federal Regulations, whichever is later. No incumbent councilmember shall be deprived of the member's unexpired term of office because of such redistricting.

(Code 1980, § 2-2-62; Ord. No. 89-46, § 12, 3-28-1989; Ord. No. 00-194, § 1(2-2-62), 11-28-2000; Ord. No. 01-91, 6-26-2001)

   III.   ENCAMPMENT ORDINANCE
          • ALANAH L. MELTON, DIRECTOR, UNHOUSED STRATEGIES
          • JULIE BARNARD, ASSISTANT CITY ATTORNEY, OFFICE OF THE CITY ATTORNEY
          Attorney Bernard presented revisions to the proposed encampment ordinance previously
          reviewed by the Council. The primary substantive revision was the addition of a definition for
          "public property," clarifying that the ordinance applies to property owned, leased, or controlled
          by state or local governmental entities.
          Questions were raised regarding enforcement on private property, zoning implications, and
          nuisance concerns.
          • Encampments on private property may violate zoning, building, and nuisance codes.
          • The Committee discussed enforcement procedures, prioritization of encampments,
              outreach efforts, notice requirements, and available resources for unhoused individuals.
          • Ms. Melton stated that the intent of the ordinance is not to criminalize homelessness but to
              connect individuals with housing and support services.
          • Discussion included:
          • storage of personal belongings,
          • best practices from other municipalities,
          • protections for important personal documents and possessions.
          • Questions were also raised regarding the authority of the Parks and Recreation Board and
              potential clarification of language related to camping permits and park regulations.
          • Penalties for violations under the municipal code were reviewed.
          Action Taken:
          No Action – Information Only

IV.   AUTHORIZATION OF ELECTRONIC MEETING ORDINANCE
      • TRACEY ROBERTS, ASSISTANT CITY ATTORNEY, OFFICE OF THE CITY ATTORNEY
      Attorney Roberts presented a proposed ordinance authorizing electronic participation in City
      Council meetings as permitted under state law.
      • Electronic participation would be permitted under specific circumstances, including illness.
      • A physical quorum would still be required.
      • The proposed ordinance limits electronic participation to six meetings per calendar year.
      • The ordinance also provides a framework for committees to adopt similar provisions.
      Action Taken:
      No Action – Information Only

V.    2026 LEGISLATIVE SESSION REPORT
      • SUSAN KENNEDY, SENIOR COUNSEL FOR EXTERNAL AFFAIRS
      Ms. Kennedy presented a summary of the City's 2026 legislative efforts and outcomes.
      Legislative Highlights:
      • Approximately 800 bills were introduced during the legislative session.
      • The City actively tracked 73 bills.
      • Four legislative priorities were proposed; two were enacted into law.
      • Several bills opposed by the City were successfully defeated, including House Bill 36,
         which would have allowed state oversight of certain municipal police departments.
      Legislative Successes:
      Vacant Property Registration Act
      • Requires registration of qualifying vacant properties.
      • Establishes escalating registration fees.
      • Provides additional tools to address blighted and abandoned properties.
      Community Land Trust Legislation
      • Establishes a framework to promote affordable homeownership opportunities.
      • Intended to support wealth-building and long-term housing stability for residents.

      The Councilors discussed implementation strategies for the Community Land Trust.

      Questions were raised regarding partnerships with housing organizations and the acquisition
      and redevelopment of vacant properties.

      Discussion also focused on enforcement mechanisms for vacant property registration.
      Future Legislative Priorities:
      • Jefferson County Land Bank legislation.
      • Alabama Housing Trust Fund legislation.
      • Renewal of Historic Tax Credits.

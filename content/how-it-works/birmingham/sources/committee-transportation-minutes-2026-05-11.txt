VI.   Parking Reform Update
      Presented by: Christine Argo
      Christine Argo, Deputy Director of the Department of Transportation, presented proposed
      amendments to the City's on-street parking ordinance as part of the department's ongoing
      parking reform initiative.

      Staff previously presented plans for a new parking management vendor and system upgrades
      and that the current recommendations represent the next phase of that effort.

      It was recommended increasing parking violation fines by $10 across the board. The City's
      parking fines have remained unchanged since 2009. The proposed increases primarily affect
      lower-dollar parking violations, while higher fines related to public safety violations would
      remain unchanged. The revised fine schedule also removes outdated and unenforceable
      provisions and reorganizes violations to better align with the current City Code and state law.

      Ms. Argo also presented a proposal to establish a $3 administrative fee for parking citations
      that remain unpaid after 30 days. The fee is intended to offset the City's costs associated with

VII.   Regional Planning Commission of Greater Birmingham MPO Update RPCGB
       Presented by: Scott Tillman, Director of Planning Operations
       Scott Tillman provided the committee with an overview of the agency's role in regional
       transportation planning and the distribution of federal transportation funding.

       The RPCGB serves six counties and 84 municipalities as the Metropolitan Planning
       Organization (MPO) for the Birmingham urbanized area. Birmingham MPO receives
       approximately $32 million annually in federal transportation funding and must approve federally
       funded transportation projects within its jurisdiction.

       An overview of the MPO's primary funding programs, including the Surface Transportation
       Block Grant (STBG), Congestion Mitigation and Air Quality (CMAQ) Program, and the
       Transportation Alternatives Program (TAP) were presented, which support roadway
       improvements, resurfacing projects, sidewalks, bicycle and pedestrian infrastructure, trails, and
       other transportation initiatives.

       Mr. Tillman also discussed upcoming federal requirements requiring transportation projects to
       be scored using a standardized prioritization system. The scoring process is intended to assist
       municipalities in evaluating proposed projects before submission while ensuring compliance
       with federal funding requirements. Municipalities will continue to establish their own project
       priorities, with the scoring system serving as an additional evaluation tool.

       All transportation funding recommendations receive review by citizen, technical, advisory, and
       policy committees before final approval.

       No Action – Information Only.
